# External inspiration library

A living list of precedents for **how** to shape experiences. It is not a source of historical facts about our places, and never a menu to copy from. Keep it apart from the project's historical sources.

**Verified** = read on the linked page on the date checked. **Principle** and **translation** are interpretations. Add entries in the same format when a precedent proves useful; mark or remove entries whose links break. Before relying on an entry older than a few months, open it again.

## Precedents

### Rider Spoke, Blast Theory (2007)
- **Link:** https://www.blasttheory.co.uk/projects/rider-spoke/ (checked 29 Oct 2026)
- **Discipline:** urban location game, participatory art.
- **What people did (verified):** cyclists rode alone at night with a phone on the handlebars; the app asked personal questions; riders hid recordings of their answers at chosen spots and searched for strangers' recordings, which played only where they'd been left.
- **Principle:** the place opens the content. A layer you can reach only by standing in one spot makes that spot matter.
- **Applications:** a short message that opens only once the group is at the stop; a line the group leaves for the next group.
- **Limitations:** solitary, night-time, GPS and a custom app. GPS must never gate a Shuk stop, so the trigger would be arrival confirmed by the group or the guide.

### Remote X, Rimini Protokoll (2013 onwards)
- **Link:** https://www.rimini-protokoll.de/website/en/project/remote-x (checked 29 Oct 2026)
- **Discipline:** immersive, participatory theatre.
- **What people did (verified):** 50 people walk a city in headphones, directed by a synthetic voice; the group watches itself choose whom to follow; binaural sound turns the city into a "collective film"; each city gets its own version.
- **Principle:** a frame (a voice, music, one instruction) can make an ordinary street look staged and worth noticing. Group decisions are content in themselves.
- **Applications:** one framing instruction at a stop ("stand still, look up, say the first thing you notice"), then the phone goes quiet and the group talks.
- **Limitations:** headphones isolate people from each other; large anonymous group; the tone doesn't suit families.

### Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes, Steel Crate Games
- **Link:** https://keeptalkinggame.com/ (checked 29 Oct 2026)
- **Discipline:** cooperative party game.
- **What people did (verified):** one player sees the bomb but has no manual; the others have the manual but can't see the bomb; only talking solves it.
- **Principle:** split the information so it can only be put together by talking.
- **Applications:** "one sees, the others know": the person facing the wall describes, the person holding the clues can't look. Fits the owner's split-clues idea.
- **Limitations:** the timer causes stress and shouting. Drop the timer; never at solemn stops.

### Stolpersteine, Gunter Demnig (1992 onwards)
- **Links:** https://www.stolpersteine.eu/en/, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stolpersteine (checked 29 Oct 2026)
- **Discipline:** memorial art, decentralised memorial.
- **What people do (verified):** small brass plates set into the pavement at a victim's last chosen home ("here lived", name, dates); found by chance; local schoolchildren, relatives and history groups research them; cleaning the brass is a community ritual. Criticised by some (people stepping on names; consent; missing context).
- **Principle:** at solemn content, offer a small act of care rather than a challenge or a score. Accuracy and the community's consent come before design.
- **Applications:** sensitive stops (Rabbi Aryeh Levin): no points, timer or quiz; one sourced line read aloud and a quiet gesture, led by the guide.
- **Limitations:** as criticised; a gesture must be offered, never forced.

### Her Long Black Hair, Janet Cardiff and George Bures Miller (2004)
- **Link:** https://www.cardiffmiller.com/walks/her-long-black-hair/ (checked 29 Oct 2026)
- **Discipline:** audio walk, interactive art.
- **What people did (verified):** a 46-minute walk through Central Park following Cardiff's voice; at points, participants hold up a photograph and compare it with the view in front of them.
- **Principle:** then-and-now at the exact viewpoint turns looking into discovery.
- **Applications:** an archival image held up at the same spot: what changed, what survived?
- **Limitations:** solo and in headphones; mixes fiction with history, which the Shuk's evidence rules don't allow. The image must be genuine and cleared (the mural's before photo is doubtful).

### [murmur], Shawn Micallef, James Roussel and Gabe Sawhney (2003 onwards)
- **Link:** https://magazine.utoronto.ca/campus/history/murmur-project-shawn-micallef-james-roussel-gabe-sawhney-toronto-oral-history/ (checked 29 Oct 2026; the project's own site didn't load)
- **Discipline:** locative oral history, heritage interpretation.
- **What people did (verified):** signs in Toronto, Vancouver and Montreal neighbourhoods showed a phone number; passers-by called and heard a one-to-two-minute story about that exact spot: changes, anecdotes, literary history.
- **Principle:** short, specific, human-voiced stories tied to one spot make an ordinary street storied.
- **Applications:** one short real voice per stop (a resident or vendor, with consent and the owner's approval), played aloud to the group.
- **Limitations:** oral stories are uncorroborated, so the wording must carry the hedge.

### Coney at heritage sites: Phoebe the Apprentice Ranger (2018) and Watching the House (2024)
- **Links:** https://coneyhq.org/project/phoebe-the-apprentice-ranger/, https://coneyhq.org/project/watching-the-house/ (checked 29 Oct 2026)
- **Discipline:** heritage interpretation, family interactive theatre.
- **What people did (verified):** at Polesden Lacey, families helped a young apprentice ranger with missions (spotting wildlife, semaphore codes). At Marble Hill House, visitors played a "Watcher" who sits quietly and observes, as the audio covers both the house's owners and the enslaved Africans whose labour furnished it; co-developed with 16-to-18-year-olds, historians and curators.
- **Principle:** give the group a role with a purpose, and match the verb to the tone: spot and decode at light stops, observe and listen at heavy ones.
- **Applications:** a real group role ("the group that notices"); a different verb per stop.
- **Limitations:** no evaluation published; a fictional character conflicts with the Shuk's no-fiction frame.

### Foods of the Lower East Side, Tenement Museum (revived 2024)
- **Link:** https://www.timeout.com/newyork/news/the-tenement-museums-food-tours-are-back-on-the-lower-east-sideafter-a-four-year-hiatus-042524 (checked 29 Oct 2026; the museum's own page returned 403)
- **Discipline:** heritage walking tour, food.
- **What people did (verified):** a 90-minute, all-ages walk with 10 stops and five tastings from local vendors, including the last remaining pickle vendor; themes of how immigrant families kept and adapted their food.
- **Principle:** one sensory act carries one historical idea; the living market is a primary source.
- **Applications:** the market and Marzipan: one shared taste framed by one question, answered aloud.
- **Limitations:** depends on vendors, cost and dietary needs; vendor stories need consent.

### Spaceteam
- **Link:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceteam (checked 29 Oct 2026; a secondary source. The studio's page, https://sleepingbeastgames.com/projects/spaceteam, gives no gameplay detail)
- **Discipline:** cooperative party game for phones.
- **What people did (verified):** two to eight players, each with a different control panel on their own device; orders appear on one player's device, but the control that carries them out is on someone else's, so the order has to be said aloud. The designer says the shouting wasn't intended: "it's the only way to getting someone to hear your instruction."
- **Principle:** put the instruction on one phone and the means on another; talking becomes the mechanic.
- **Applications:** complementary puzzle parts, e.g. each vessel of the well on a different phone, so the group pours by talking.
- **Limitations:** short timers and shouting in harder levels; drop both. A quiet residential street isn't a party room.

### Captain Sonar
- **Link:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Sonar (checked 29 Oct 2026; secondary source)
- **Discipline:** team board game.
- **What people did (verified):** two teams of up to four sit on either side of a divider, each filling four roles; the 2–4 player version merges the Captain and First Mate.
- **Principle:** design roles so they merge cleanly when fewer people play.
- **Applications:** mission parts that combine on one phone in a small team; the same mission works for two players or eight.
- **Limitations:** real-time and competitive by design; the article doesn't describe how the roles depend on each other.

### Hanabi
- **Link:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanabi_(card_game) (checked 29 Oct 2026; secondary source)
- **Discipline:** cooperative card game.
- **What people did (verified):** players "can see each other's cards but they cannot see their own", and pass limited, exact clues paid for with tokens.
- **Principle:** you can hold information meant for someone else; helping means telling them.
- **Applications:** a clue card that names what your neighbour must find on the wall.
- **Limitations:** quiet, slow deduction; on a busy street keep it to one exchange, not a chain.

### Pandemic
- **Link:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandemic_(board_game) (checked 29 Oct 2026; secondary source)
- **Discipline:** cooperative board game.
- **What people did (verified):** each player is dealt a random role; roles "allow them to alter the above rules" (the Medic treats every cube in a city with one action; the Scientist cures with four cards instead of five).
- **Principle:** a role is an ability, not a title. If a role doesn't change what someone can see or do, it is decoration.
- **Applications:** the test for any proposed game role; titles without a different view or action stay out.
- **Limitations:** roles are dealt at random and fixed for the game; families need to choose, swap and skip roles.

## Cross-cutting principles (interpretation)

1. The place opens the content.
2. Information split between people so they must talk.
3. Short, local, human-voiced stories beat textbook text.
4. Then-and-now at the same viewpoint.
5. Match the verb to the tone; solemn stops get acts of care, not scores.
6. Technology frames the moment, then goes quiet.
7. Facilitators matter for family learning; surprise and social interaction help people learn.
8. A role is an ability, not a title, and roles must merge cleanly when few people play.
