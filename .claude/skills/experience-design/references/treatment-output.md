# What a full treatment covers

When asked to develop an educational treatment, a complete answer usually covers the points below. Scale it down for smaller requests: often a short paragraph per point, or only the points that matter.

1. **Raw knowledge and source material.** The facts, people, conflicts, surprises, artifacts and emotional themes, each with its source and evidence status. What is physically present at the place today. Gaps and open questions.
2. **Central educational insight.** The one idea worth carrying away, stated as something a person could disagree with, not a topic.
3. **The human hook.** The curiosity gap or emotional pull that makes people *want* to know. Often the most surprising true detail.
4. **Alternative experience concepts.** Three or more meaningfully different concepts, each in two or three lines: what people do, what they feel, where the reveal lands. Name the external precedents or principles that inspired them, if any.
5. **Recommended treatment and rationale.** Which concept, and why it fits this content, place and audience better than the others. What it gives up.
6. **Moment-by-moment journey.** The sequence of actions, reactions, interactions and reveals, including what is withheld until when, when the phone comes out and goes away, and the bridge to the next stop.
7. **Multiplayer and physical-world interactions.** Who holds or does what; how people need each other; what the place contributes.
8. **The intended memorable moment.** The one moment they'll retell, and why it is earned.
9. **Educational payoff.** What they can now understand, interpret, appreciate or remember; how the interaction carries it.
10. **Feasibility, dependencies and risks.** Field checks, sources, copy, rights, consent, vendors, timing, accessibility, failure modes, the fallback path without camera, microphone or GPS, and what needs the owner's decision.

Mark every proposed participant-facing line as draft. Mark every invented game device as invented.
