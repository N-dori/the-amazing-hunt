# Quality criteria and anti-patterns

Use these to critique a treatment (step 8), not to score it. A treatment can be strong on most and deliberately quiet on one, such as a solemn stop with little play. Say which trade-offs are intended.

## Criteria

| Criterion | The question to ask | Strong looks like | Weak looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Educational meaning** | What will people understand, interpret or appreciate afterwards that they couldn't before? | The action *is* the understanding: doing it teaches the idea | The interaction tests recall of something just read |
| **Curiosity and discovery** | Where is the open question, and who closes it? | People want to know before they're told; they find it themselves | The answer arrives before anyone wondered |
| **Emotional engagement** | What should people feel, and why does the material earn it? | Delight, awe, tenderness or gravity that comes from the true story | Excitement pasted on; a cheer that ignores the content |
| **Social interaction** | How do people create value for each other? | Talk is needed; each person holds something; ages mix | One person taps while the others wait |
| **Connection to place** | What here makes this impossible anywhere else? | People look up, walk, find, touch; the place answers | The phone holds everything; the street is scenery |
| **Memorability** | What will they tell someone at dinner tonight? | One clear, earned moment that sticks | Many small facts, none remembered |
| **Accessibility and mixed ages** | Can a grandparent, a teen and a seven-year-old all take part, with no sight, hearing, mobility or language barrier left without a path? | Several ways in; roles of different difficulty; a text or guide path for every sensor | Small text, fast timing, one modality, reading age too high |
| **Practical feasibility** | Will it work on a hot, noisy street, with weak signal, in 5 to 10 minutes, with what we have? | Low setup; graceful fallbacks; no vendor or crowd dependency left unplanned | Needs hardware, perfect GPS, permissions or a vendor's goodwill we don't have |

## Anti-patterns

Each comes with the sign that you're in it and a way out.

- **Quiz after every story.** *Sign:* read, then multiple choice, then reveal, at stop after stop. *Way out:* move the question before the story (predict), into the place (find it), or between people (they need each other's pieces). Some stops need no question at all.
- **Puzzles unrelated to the content.** *Sign:* the puzzle would work with any other story swapped in. *Way out:* derive the puzzle from a real problem in the story: what the original people had to solve, count, choose or notice.
- **Badges or collectibles without meaningful payoff.** *Sign:* you can't say what an item *means* or what collecting it changes. *Way out:* each object carries a piece of the story's meaning, and together they produce something worth having at the end. Otherwise drop them.
- **Everyone silently looking at their own phone.** *Sign:* heads down, no talking. *Way out:* the phone sets up a moment and then gets out of the way; the answer is out there or in someone else's hand.
- **Technology because it's impressive.** *Sign:* the feature is the selling point; the experience would be the same without the content. *Way out:* ask what job only the digital layer can do here (show the invisible, hold shared state, give each person a different piece). If none, leave it out.
- **Forced excitement around serious topics.** *Sign:* points, timers, cheers or jokes next to death, prisoners, grief or faith. *Way out:* quiet, untimed, unscored; let the place and the story carry the weight; give a gentle way to respond or reflect.
- **Could happen anywhere.** *Sign:* the hotel-room test passes. *Way out:* find what is physically present and put it at the centre of the task.
- **Variety for its own sake.** *Sign:* a different mechanic at each stop, chosen from a list rather than from the story. *Way out:* let each stop's character come from its material; repeat a format when it truly fits.
- **One mechanic everywhere.** *Sign:* the same interaction (split clues, multiple choice, a prediction) at stop after stop, because it worked once or the project favours it. *Way out:* check the neighbouring stops; choose the kind of participation this material asks for; keep the favoured mechanic for the stops where it carries the meaning.
- **The text wall.** *Sign:* people read long passages aloud while standing in the sun. *Way out:* one idea per screen; move detail into the reveal or optional depth; let the guide tell what is better told.
- **Disguised test.** *Sign:* the "challenge" is just checking whether they read the paragraph. *Way out:* ask something they must look, reason, compare or decide to answer.
