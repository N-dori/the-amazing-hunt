# Researching external inspiration

Project knowledge tells us *what* to tell. Research into how others have shaped experiences widens *how* we could tell it. This is about presentation, never about historical facts: a precedent never becomes a source for a claim about our places.

## When

- **Small requests** (copy, a hint, an order change): skip it.
- **New mission, major redesign, new interaction format, game-wide concept:** do it when web access is available.
- **No web access:** use the [inspiration library](inspiration-library.md) and the project's references, and say plainly that no fresh research was performed.

Never describe a site, project or case study you did not actually open in this session or find recorded in the library with a date checked.

## Where to look

Look well beyond educational technology. The best ideas often come from fields with nothing to do with education:

- immersive theatre and participatory performance;
- theme park and attraction design;
- escape rooms and alternate reality games;
- interactive documentaries and journalism;
- museum and science-centre exhibitions;
- location-based adventures and urban games;
- cooperative board games and party games;
- experiential storytelling and narrative design;
- interactive art installations;
- playful learning and behavioural design;
- cultural heritage interpretation;
- children's museums and family experiences.

Starting points are in the [inspiration library](inspiration-library.md). They are a start, not a fence: find new creators, studios, research, games, exhibitions and installations.

## For each promising precedent

Prefer primary sources and detailed case studies, and link them.

1. What did participants actually do?
2. What made it compelling?
3. Where did curiosity, surprise, emotional investment or social interaction emerge?
4. What role did the physical environment play?
5. What was the relationship between the participant and the technology?
6. What design principle made it work?
7. What limitations or failures can be identified?
8. How could that principle become a simpler, original experience for our material?

Keep **verified** (read on the page) apart from **interpretation** (your reading of why it worked) and **assumption**.

## Translate principles, don't copy mechanics

Not: "Disney uses interactive bounty hunts, so we add a bounty hunt."
But: "Disney makes visitors feel an ordinary-looking environment holds a hidden layer. Can authentic historical details in a real street give that feeling of secret discovery?"

Not: "A museum lets visitors interrogate objects, so every object should talk."
But: "Giving objects a perspective turns information into an encounter. What else could make this knowledge feel personal and alive?"

## Cross-pollinate on big assignments

- Look at several meaningfully different precedents.
- Name the principle behind each.
- Try combining two or more principles.
- Propose original treatments grounded in the actual material.
- Check whether the result is more meaningful than simply telling the story well. Sometimes it isn't, and the best treatment is a great story told at the right spot.
- Don't default to the most technically impressive precedent.

## Keep the library alive

When a precedent proves useful, add it to the [inspiration library](inspiration-library.md) in the same format, with the date checked. Remove or mark entries whose links break or that stop being useful.
