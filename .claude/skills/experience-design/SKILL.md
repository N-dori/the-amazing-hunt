# Educational experience design

> **How can we turn what we know into something people will love experiencing?**

This is a way of thinking, not a form to fill in. Start from the material and the human experience it could become; choose mechanics last. Use as much of it as the request deserves (see "Depth"), and feel free to break it when a better idea needs room.

**Our project knowledge tells us what stories we have to tell. External creative research expands our imagination of how they can be experienced. Experience design connects the two.**

## The process

Move through these in order the first time; loop back whenever a later step exposes a weak earlier one.

1. **Understand the raw material.** Read the actual sources, not a summary. Pull out the facts, people, conflicts, surprises, artifacts, places and emotional themes, each with its evidence status (fact, interpretation, tradition, unknown). Note what is *physically there today*. Ask what the material is really about: the topic is a cistern; the meaning may be scarcity, honour or dependence.
2. **Find the experiential opportunities.** Where could people *discover, notice, feel, do, decide or be surprised* instead of being told? Look for the hidden layer in something ordinary, the moment a choice was made, the detail that's easy to miss, the sensory trace, the question the original people faced, the thing only a group together could figure out. The best opportunities are usually already inside the true story.
3. **Explore multiple treatments.** Sketch at least three *meaningfully different* concepts (different in what people do and feel, not in skin). For substantial work, research precedents from unrelated disciplines first ([research method](references/external-research.md), [inspiration library](references/inspiration-library.md)). Extract principles, never copy mechanics. Only then glance at a mechanic catalogue to name or refine what you have.
4. **Design the participant journey.** Moment by moment: what people see, do, say to each other, feel, and what is withheld until when. Where the phone comes out, and where it goes back in a pocket. Where the reveal lands. How the stop hands over to the next one.
5. **Design the group dynamics.** How does each person create value for the others? Who holds what, who needs whom, how do a grandparent and a nine-year-old both matter? Avoid the pattern where one person plays and the rest watch.
6. **Connect to the physical world.** Use the real place wherever it makes the experience better: things to find, read, touch, measure, smell, stand in, walk through. Test: *would this work just as well in a hotel room?* If yes, it isn't using the place.
7. **Evaluate the educational value.** After it, what can people understand, interpret, appreciate or remember that they couldn't before? Is the memorable moment the same as the meaningful one? Does the interaction carry the content, or sit beside it?
8. **Critique the experience.** Run the [criteria and anti-patterns](references/quality-criteria.md) honestly. Challenge generic gamification, needless screen time, artificial puzzles, superficial engagement and forced excitement. Cut what doesn't earn its place.
9. **Recommend a treatment.** Say why this approach fits *this* content, *this* place, *this* audience and *this point in the journey* better than the alternatives, and what it costs. Offer the runner-up when it's close.
10. **Translate into implementation only when asked.** When it's time for UX or code, carry the creative intent forward explicitly (what the moment must feel like, what must stay physical, what must stay withheld) so it survives into specs. Then the project's own build loop takes over.

The [output structure](references/treatment-output.md) lists what a full treatment usually covers. Use it as a checklist for thinking, not as a document to produce every time.

## Depth

Match the effort to the request:

- **Small** (a line of copy, one hint, a reveal's order): steps 1, 2 and 8 in your head; answer briefly. No external research.
- **Medium** (improving one existing mission's engagement): all steps, two or three concepts, existing references; external research if one fresh angle would help.
- **Large** (a new mission, a major redesign, a new interaction format, the game-wide arc): all steps, external research when web access is available, cross-pollination of several precedents, a full treatment.

When no web research was done, say so plainly. Never claim to have looked at a site or case study you didn't open.

## Principles to hold onto

- **The place is the centre.** Place first, the human guide second, the digital layer third. Technology serves the experience, never the reverse.
- **Discovery before explanation.** A reveal without a prior question, prediction or effort is just a fact.
- **Every stop has its own character**, and **one line runs through the whole experience**. Variety comes from the material, not from rotating mechanics for their own sake.
- **Judge a mechanic across the journey, not only at its stop.** A mechanic that is excellent for one mission becomes repetitive when every mission uses it. Before recommending, look at the nearby stops and the patterns already built, and vary what people do, feel and how they move: observation, prediction, reconstruction, creative expression, conversation, sensory discovery, reflective storytelling, as well as split clues. Don't default to a mechanic because the project has praised it, and never trade historical meaning for variety.
- **The model:** knowledge → story → an interaction that changes from stop to stop and brings the *whole group* in → discovery, a memorable moment, a surprise.
- **Story first, mechanic last.** Find the experiential potential hidden inside the actual history before deciding on any mechanic.
- **Truth is not negotiable.** External inspiration shapes *how* we present knowledge, never *what* the facts are. Inventions (a game device, a puzzle) are marked as such.
- **Respect sets the register.** Serious or sensitive stories get quiet, dignified treatments; not every stop should be fun in the same way.
- **Keep it open.** These are today's best ideas, not permanent rules. Experiment, notice what players actually do, and improve.
