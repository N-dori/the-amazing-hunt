# Nahlaot & Mahane Yehuda — Mission & Task Ideas

Oct 23, 2026 · @Nadav

A working bank of mission and task ideas for the Nahlaot and Mahane Yehuda game, built from the field photos, the story index and the engagement research. Ideas here are raw material, not approved missions.

## How to use this file

**Every idea follows one rule: one clue on the phone, one physical action, one discovery, one reveal.** If an idea can be solved by reading the phone alone, it doesn't belong here.

- **Evidence tag** says how far the story behind the task can be trusted: **DOC** documented · **FIELD** photographed on site, 23 Oct 2026 (photo # from `photo_index_ALL.csv`) · **ORAL** guide or family story · **FOLK** folklore, told as "the story goes".
- **Team format:** **Race** (teams compete on the same task) · **Split** (each team holds half the clues and must trade) · **Joint** (both teams together) · **Vote** (teams decide, then the real outcome is revealed).
- **Concept fit:** which concept from the [rethinking report](https://claude.ai/artifact/2Bnq8Ddh4tPf7KzyAajsnj) the idea suits best: **Files** (The Neighborhood Files), **Committee** (The Courtyard Committee), **Pots** (Whose Pot Is It?). Most ideas work in more than one.
- **Status:** Idea → Shortlisted → Paper-tested → Field-tested → Dropped. Everything starts as Idea.
- **Add freely.** New ideas go in the Parking lot first; move them into the bank once they have a place, an action and a reveal.

## Game start: name and onboarding

**Name (Nadav, 24 Oct 2026): The Amazing Shuk.** It nods to the TV show without copying the format name. Check the trademark before printing or selling it; "The Amazing Race" is a CBS format.

**Start sequence (about 3 minutes, before the opening mural)**

1. **Title screen:** The Amazing Shuk.
2. **Teams:** the family chooses 1, 2 or 3 teams (or one group), with team names.
3. **How the app works**, one short slide each, swipe to skip:
   - **The map:** stops appear on an illustrated map. When you arrive at a stop, tap its icon.
   - **A task:** the icon opens a task page with a preview. Some tasks tell the story first and then set the task; others set the task first and reveal the story after.
   - **Food:** the market menu, with stalls, dishes and their token prices.
   - **Points and tokens:** each task earns team points (scores revealed at the end) and food tokens (spent on food for everyone).
4. **Go:** the first stop, the painted market mural, unlocks on the map.

**Story first or task first?** A working rule: put the task first when the story would give away the answer (gematria, "what day is it?", Rivka's roses); put the story first when players need it to do the task (the cistern and the 4-litre puzzle, Hesed VeRahamim's two stories). Mark each task with its order in the task file.

**Note:** that makes four how-it-works slides (map, task, food, points). If it must be three, merge food and points into one slide.

## Opening task: the painted market

**Chosen (Nadav, 24 Oct 2026): the game opens at the giant trompe-l'oeil mural on Agrippas St, next to HaRav Shmuel Baruch St.** It shows the market the players are about to enter, so the first task is to look closely before the real thing begins. Field photos: three, taken 24 Oct 2026 (to add to the photo index as #103–105).

**What the mural shows (from the photos)**

- **Top left:** a market alley under stone arches, strings of Israeli flags, a dense crowd, fruit piles, sacks, watermelons; behind it a hillside city of stone houses.
- **Top right:** two painted balconies with blue shutters. A woman hangs red, white and green laundry; a man on the lower balcony reaches up beside a bicycle.
- **Bottom (the ground-floor kiosk):** a painted shop under a red scalloped awning: a man on the phone behind a counter of salads, a pile of pita, strings of garlic; to the left two boys and a café scene.
- **Real details mixed in:** the house-number tile "103", real air vents set into the painted awning and shop, a small plaque left of the painted shop, a blue sign with a treble clef on the right (possibly a "שיר בשוק" poem sign).

**How it plays (about 8 min, plus 3–4 min per bonus round)**

1. **Go there.** "Go to Agrippas St 103, by the corner of HaRav Shmuel Baruch St. Look for a market that isn't there."
2. **Find the details.** Each team gets 3 close-up crops, different for each team: e.g. the man on the phone, the bicycle on the balcony, the laundry, the boy holding a drink, the garlic strings, the man in the red cap. They find each one and photograph it; the guide confirms.
3. **Real or painted?** "Find 2 things on this wall that are real, not painted." Answers: the air vents, the number tile 103, the plaque, the blue sign.
4. **Reveal.** One line on the mural, then the hook for the whole game: "Some of what you'll see today is real and some is painted over. Your job is to tell which is which."
5. **Reward.** First points and first food tokens of the game.

**Bonus rounds (pick one or two, depending on time)**

- **Date the painting (Nadav).** Look at the man behind the counter: his phone has an antenna, it isn't a smartphone. When was this painted? Before smartphones, roughly the 2000s. Fits the 2007 lead; the plaque will confirm.
- **Name the food (Nadav).** Name as many Israeli foods as you can on the painted counter and in the market scene: stacked pita or laffa, salad bowls (purple cabbage, orange and green salads, olives), round pastries in the display case, garlic strings, sacks of spices, watermelons, pomegranates. One point per food the guide accepts. Reveal: "You'll taste some of these today; earn the tokens first." Ties the opener to the food tokens.
- **What day is it? (Nadav, upper mural).** The mural shows a typical day of the week; which one? Clues: laundry on the balcony, flowers on the railings, a man reaching up at his window, a packed market below. Reveal, the old Jerusalem household week: Wednesday is cleaning day (laundry, washing, watering the flowers), Thursday is shopping day, Friday you cook until Shabbat begins. Both answers can be argued (laundry says Wednesday, the crowded market says Thursday), so teams defend their pick. Tag: ORAL; the Thursday and Friday shopping peak is documented (05a §5.8).

* **What's missing? (Nadav).** The app shows a photo of the mural with several elements digitally removed (e.g. the bicycle, the laundry, the man on the phone, a watermelon). Teams compare it with the real wall and name every missing piece. One point per item found. Build the altered image from your own photo.

**Why it works as an opener:** no reading, instant success for all ages, it teaches the "find the detail" mechanic, and "real or painted?" sets up the whole game's theme (true story or retold one?).

**To check on site**

- Read the plaque left of the painted shop: it likely names the artists and the year.
- Confirm the address: the photo shows a "103" tile, but Agrippas numbering and the corner need checking.
- Unconfirmed lead: a giant wall painting by the French team CitéCréation was unveiled "near Mahane Yehuda" in January 2007 (masa.co.il); the trompe-l'oeil style fits, but don't state it until the plaque confirms.
- Crops: make them from your own photos; the mural's image rights may belong to the artists, so check before using crops in a published app.

## Game structure: earn tokens, spend when hungry

**Decided (Nadav, 23 Oct 2026): two separate counts, team points and shared food tokens.** Every solved task earns the team **points** (for the competition, kept per team and revealed at the end) and **food tokens**. Food tokens buy tastings for **the whole family**, not just the team that earned them; the earning team decides what everyone tastes and when. Since the guide pays on the spot, food never depends on which team is winning: everyone eats together, and the competition lives in the points.

1. **Earn.** Tasks in Ohel Moshe, Mazkeret Moshe and Batei Goral pay the team points (base for finishing, bonus for the optional puzzle) and food tokens for the family. Hints cost a point.
2. **Check the menu.** The phone shows the tasting menu: each stall near Agrippas St, the dish, its story in one line, and its price in tokens.
3. **Decide.** The team that earned the food tokens chooses what the whole family tastes: spend now on a small taste for everyone, or save for something bigger later.
4. **Spend.** Walk to the stall, claim the tasting; the guide pays on the spot and deducts the tokens. Some claims include a counter task (e.g. name the kubbeh, guess where the sesame comes from).
5. **Repeat** until time is up. Unspent tokens can buy one final shared dish at the closing table, where the family question (idea 30) ends the game.

**Route zone (Nadav, 23 Oct 2026):** most tasks sit in **Ohel Moshe, Mazkeret Moshe and Batei Goral**; tastings are at stalls **close to Agrippas St**, so the crossing leads straight to food.

**Why it works**

- **Hunger handled by design:** teams eat when they need to, which suits grandparents and teens alike.
- **A real decision every round:** spend now or save; one big dish or several small tastes.
- **Short hops, not long crossings:** Mazkeret Moshe and Ohel Moshe sit just across Agrippas St from the market's edge, so a food run is a few minutes (walking times still to measure).
- **Food is earned, not given:** each taste is the visible payoff of a solved puzzle, and is linked to a story where possible.

**What to watch**

- **Hunger and fatigue:** an hour of walking before any food is long for grandparents. Plan a water stop, or one small "advance" token mid-route.
- **No team goes hungry:** every team earns enough base tokens for a full tasting; bonus tokens add choice, not food. The DORI research warns against punishing the losing side.
- **Stories still need a home:** market ideas (3–6, 8, 10, 11) move into the claim phase as short counter tasks, or are dropped.
- **Vendor setup:** for a family group the guide pays on the spot (pre-paid arrangements are only for big groups), so a token is a promise the guide redeems at the counter, not a voucher the vendor must recognise. Agree in advance only which stalls and which tastes; avoid Friday peak hours.
- **Fit with the stories:** where possible, link a token to a stall whose family story was heard on the way (e.g. Kurdish kubbeh after the Kurdish mosaic).

**To design next**

- **Token prices:** set so a team that solves every base task can afford about 4–5 tastes; since food is shared, a team with a weak score still eats; set the balance so every team gets at least one turn choosing what the family tastes.
- **The menu page:** 6–8 stalls close to Agrippas St, each with dish, price, one-line story and opening hours; build it once the stalls are confirmed.
- **Time box:** a total game clock (e.g. 90 minutes) so teams don't spend the whole time eating; a soft reminder at 60 minutes.
- **Both teams at one stall:** allowed; first come, first served, or the guide splits the order.
- **Cost control:** the guide holds the budget; a per-team cap in shekels sits behind the token prices.

**How to play Samal (idea 23).** Rules from the Drive file `סמל וסוס ארוך.doc`, via the Drive extract (§1.3).

1. Draw a start line on the ground.
2. One player is the "donkey": they stand just behind the line and bend over, hands on knees.
3. The others take turns: run up from the line, put both hands on the donkey's back and leapfrog over, shouting "Samal!".
4. After everyone has jumped, the donkey moves one step further from the line, so the next jump is longer.
5. Whoever fails to clear the jump, or touches the ground before landing, becomes the new donkey.

**For this game:** teens jump; grandparents judge clean jumps and call each step. One round of about 5 minutes. Use soft ground away from benches, skip it if anyone is unsteady, and keep the shouting quiet (residential garden). Team version: each successful jump earns a point for the jumper's team.

**Sus Arokh (the "long horse") is too rough for a mixed-age group:** one team forms a bent-over chain and the other jumps onto their backs to collapse it. Mention it as part of the song's story, but don't play it.

**How to play the water puzzle (idea 24).** Tell the cistern story at a courtyard (one roof-fed cistern, every family's water). Then: "Your family may take exactly 4 litres. You have a 3-litre jug and a 5-litre jug, nothing else."

1. Fill the 5 L jug.
2. Pour from it into the 3 L jug until full (2 L left in the 5).
3. Empty the 3 L jug.
4. Pour the 2 L into the 3 L jug.
5. Fill the 5 L jug again.
6. Top up the 3 L jug (it takes 1 L): exactly 4 L remain in the 5.

**Physical version:** bring two cups marked 300 ml and 500 ml and a bottle of water; target 400 ml. Same logic, light to carry, and nobody pours into a real cistern. Teams race; the guide checks the level. **Hint ladder:** (1) "What if you fill the big one first?" (2) "What's left in the 5 after you fill the 3?" Each hint costs a token.

## Mechanics menu

**From the engagement research, 14 mechanics suit a walking phone game in these streets, 6 need care, and 7 groups should be avoided.** Numbers refer to the mechanics list in *audience\_engagement\_and\_interactive\_digital\_experiences.md*; idea numbers refer to the bank above.

**Play modes (Nadav, 23 Oct 2026): every task must work with 1, 2 or 3 teams.** The guide sets the number at the start: three teams (e.g. three pairs), two teams, or one group when a family wants to play all together. With three teams: Race is three-way; Split clues come in three parts, so all three must trade; Vote is by majority; points are kept per team, and each team spends its food tokens on food for everyone.

| Team format in the bank | Two teams (default) | One group (whole family) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Race | Teams race each other | Race a target: a par time or par score per task, set by the guide |
| Split | Each team holds half the clues and must trade | Split inside the family: each generation gets a different clue on its own phone, or the guide hands one clue to the grandparents and one to the teens |
| Vote | Teams vote, then the real outcome | Everyone votes on their own phone; the split between generations becomes the discussion |
| Joint | Both teams together | Unchanged |
| Tokens | Points per team; food tokens are spent by the earning team on food for everyone | One shared wallet; the family decides together when to spend |
| Score | Team totals revealed at the end | Score against the par for the route ("you beat 6 of 8 targets"); no rival needed |

## App fit: the existing trivia engine

**Checked 24 Oct 2026 (Claude Code, read-only review of the game repo): the existing multiplayer trivia engine does not fit the walking game as it stands; reuse it only for a closing-table quiz.** It is a live quiz show: one admin sets the pace, everyone answers the same question within 30 seconds, and all game state lives in one server's memory.

| The walking game needs | The engine does | Gap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1, 2 or 3 teams with shared team state | One player per phone, no teams | Structural |
| Each team at its own pace, at different stops | Same question for everyone, paced by the admin | Structural |
| Answers found in the street (count, name, photo) | 30-second timer; multiple choice and true/false only | Structural |
| Survive weak signal in the lanes | A reconnect creates a new player with 0 points; the timer keeps running | Blocker |
| Team points plus shared food tokens and a tasting menu | None | Build |
| Guide confirms finds, adjusts points, records spending | Admin can only start the game and move to the next question | Build |
| Scores hidden until the end | Leaderboard after every question | Easy fix |
| Easy start for a family | Login needed to create or join; room name typed by hand | Friction |
| Hebrew and English | Hebrew content, RTL in CSS; no `dir` on `<html>` | Small fix |
| Adding tasks without a developer | Questions hard-coded in TS/JS; IDs change on every load | Fix before reuse |

**Worth reusing:** the question data format (image, category, difficulty, ordering type), login and the Supabase project, the responsive layout, and the live quiz itself for one moment: the closing table, where all teams sit together with signal. A 30-second round on what they found fits well there.

**Recommended (not yet decided):** build the walking game as a new, simpler module beside the trivia engine:

1. Team state saved in Supabase (team, points, food tokens, tasks done, spending), so a reconnect simply reloads the team.
2. Each team's phone works on its own and syncs when it has signal.
3. Task types: number, text, choice, order, and photo with guide confirmation.
4. A guide screen: approve finds, adjust points and tokens, record what was bought.
5. Tasks in one JSON file with fixed IDs, so new ideas from this bank don't need code changes.

Source: Claude Code's review of the repo, files cited there (`backend/src/sockets/state.js`, `sockets/index.js`, `routes/trivia.js`, `assets/data/*Data.tsx`, `supabase/init.sql`, `layout.tsx`).
