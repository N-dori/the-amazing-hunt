## Using the Endowment Effect Creatively

…endowment effect creatively.

The first way is through **customization**.

In apps like games or social media, you'll often see this right away: asking users to pick a nickname, an avatar, or a theme early on. That helps build an emotional connection from the start.

Instagram has new users add friends from Facebook and their contacts before they even land on the Instagram Explore page. This can quickly create a sense of ownership and belonging.

But for more practical tools, like ones focused on productivity or efficiency, people often want to dive in and get to work first. In those cases, customization can still play a role later on and can help your users work faster and make the tool feel more like their own.

Think of Google Chrome's themes, custom bookmarks, or shortcuts. These tiny acts of choice signal: **“This is mine.”**

## Interactive Previews

The next way is through **interactive previews**.

Amazon has an artificial reality feature that allows you to see products, such as sofas or tables, in your room. This is powerful because it encourages users to visualize the item as theirs.

## History and Personal Investment

A third way is through **history**.

Cloud storage tools are great at this. When you cancel, sometimes they'll remind you of what you're losing: **your photos, your memories**. And this helps to remind users about what they've built, saved, and customized.

Using previews, trials, or customization can trigger that sense of ownership. But as always, it is crucial to be honest.

**Do not emotionally manipulate users with deceptive design patterns.** The goal is always to create genuine value, not to exploit your customers' emotions.

## When You Want to Break the Attachment

Other times, you *do* want to break the attachment.

Consider situations when you're encouraging users to switch to your tool or to upgrade their plan. In those cases, you may need to break the attachment and sense of ownership.

Understanding the endowment effect can help you do this.

Rather than leaning into emotions, present the logic. Highlight the limits of what they currently have. Focus on clear, objective benefits of your alternative, and make the transition feel easy and low effort.

## Closing Takeaway

The endowment effect shows that even small touches of ownership can go a long way. Use it thoughtfully to build experiences that your users will truly want to keep.
