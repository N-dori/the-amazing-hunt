# Time, Order and Estimates: Ordering, Chronology, Number and Slider

**Status:** research synthesis, 4 Nov 2026; pending owner decision.

## Bottom line

For history with children, **putting images in order is more developmentally appropriate than dates**. Estimates are best as "look, guess, then see the real value", which is an observation tool rather than a memory trick. Neither format proves causal understanding, so add a "why" prompt.

## Ordering (`ANS-ORDER`)

| Finding | Label |
|---|---|
| 58 children (kindergarten to 6th grade) ordered historical pictures with real agreement at all ages; "dates had little meaning for children before third grade"; most linked dates to knowledge only by 5th grade | OBSERVED: [Barton & Levstik 1996](https://www.thenhier.ca/en/node/318.html) |
| Forward sequencing of familiar events is easier for young children than backward or unfamiliar sequences | OBSERVED per the GPT run; source not re-checked |
| Learning benefit of ordering tasks in the field | UNKNOWN |

**Rules (INFERRED):**
- 3 visual states (oldest → middle → today), or the stages of a visible process.
- Lets non-typists contribute.
- Follow it with a cause prompt ("what changed and why?").
- Chance of guessing an order of n items is 1/n!; for 3 items, 1/6.
- Actionbound and Explorama support sort lists; Questo shows image reordering (OBSERVED).

## Estimates (`ANS-NUM`) and sliders (`ANS-SLIDER`)

| Finding | Label |
|---|---|
| Children predicting outcomes remembered more than baseline, but learned from wrong predictions only with stronger inhibitory control | OBSERVED: [Brod et al. 2020](https://www.pedocs.de/volltexte/2021/23315/pdf/Developmental_Science_2020_3_Brod_et_al_Being_proven_wrong_A.pdf) |
| Pre-questions improve memory for the asked fact | OBSERVED: [Pan & Carpenter 2023](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10648-023-09814-5) |
| Children's estimation accuracy grows with age, mixing visual and counting strategies (longitudinal, 350 children) | OBSERVED per the GPT run; source not re-checked |
| Estimation during heritage walks improving long-term learning | UNKNOWN |

**Rules (INFERRED):**
- Estimate something **visible** (arches, steps, stalls, the age of a building), then reveal the real value with the evidence.
- Score with a tolerance band, not exact hits.
- Make the correction explicit for children.
- Actionbound's number line with tolerance (10% of points lost per 10 units off, in its example) is a documented precedent (OBSERVED).
- Slider precision is a UX risk; see prompt 3 (pending).

## Unknown
