# Collections Across the Route: Accumulated Evidence

**Status:** research synthesis, 4 Nov 2026. Owner direction (lions, tzedakah boxes, Hanukkah menorahs), pending decision. Proposed format ID: `ANS-COLLECT`.

## Bottom line

A collection that runs through the whole route is one of the best-supported directions in this pack. Three effects stack up:
- the concept comes back again and again (spacing);
- families compare different examples (category learning);
- a progress counter keeps them going (motivation).

The catches:
- **Lions are already taken nearby.** A Hebrew family phone game runs a lion hunt from Mahane Yehuda along Jaffa Road.
- **Menorahs are seasonal.**
- **How many tzedakah boxes are actually on the route is unknown** until someone walks it.

## The mechanic (INFERRED)

1. **Introduce once, in full.** At the first example: what it is, why it's here, and a reveal. For tzedakah: what a tzedakah box is, before any hunt.
2. **Later finds are quick:** spot it, tap or photograph the detail, and get one new fact or one difference. No full challenge each time.
3. **Every find adds something.** "This one has two slots", "this lion has wings". A count with no new content is just a checklist.
4. **Compare at the end.** Sort the collection, find what they share, then answer a final question from the evidence. Example: "Why does Jerusalem put lions everywhere?" This is the *accumulated evidence* step.
5. **Souvenir:** the family's own collection gallery.

| A collection improving learning on heritage walks | UNKNOWN; no direct study found |

**Design reading (INFERRED):**
- **Pick different-looking examples:** a mural, a stone carving, a manhole cover. Near-copies teach less.
- **Explain the first example before the hunt starts.** Comparison fails on unfamiliar examples.
- **Count the introduction as find no. 1** (endowed progress).
- **Ask for a detail at each find,** not the whole object.

**Implication (INFERRED):**
- **Tzedakah boxes** could be the year-round core, if the route walk confirms enough of them. Nobody on the route is doing them.
- **Lions** work better as a strand inside a larger story than as the main game, because "There's a Lion Here!" already sells exactly that as the whole game.
- **Menorahs** suit a **Hanukkah seasonal edition.**

## How to check a find

- **Known, fixed examples:** GPS plus matching against a reference photo (see Run 10b).
- **"Any lion you can find":** the hardest case to auto-check. Use self-confirm (`ANS-SELF`), or an LLM yes/no on the photo with a fallback.
- **Open-ended counts** ("found 5 of at least 7") so a missing or painted-over example never blocks the route.

## Group play

- Spotting suits non-readers and younger children; joint attention is built in (see 06).
- **Risk:** the fastest spotter takes over. Use turns ("this one is Noa's find") or one collection per child.
