# Memo: what DORI can learn from city-quest competitors

**To:** the owner · **Date:** 3 Nov 2026 · **Status:** research proposals, pending your decisions
**Basis:** 18 competitor profiles, 22 × 45 comparison matrix, 107 sources (`DORI_Research_Library.xlsx`; full detail in `DORI_Phase4_Synthesis.md`)

## Bottom line
- **What the research can and can't do:** it is strong enough to frame tests, not to make product decisions.
  - 84% of coded evidence comes from vendor documentation.
  - Nothing has been played or field-tested.
  - The two competitors in Mahane Yehuda are the least documented.
- **Where DORI's principles sit in the field:** they line up with the weakest parts of competitors' public evidence (place use, labelled history, recovery). That suggests room to stand apart, but nothing shows yet that visitors value it or will pay for it.

## Eight conclusions
1. **Place dependence is the clearest possible differentiator; its value to players is unproven.** Eskap reuses one game across 60+ cities and a reviewer says it barely uses the place. Only one demo (Geocaching Adventure Lab) shows tasks that truly need the place. *(C-01)*
2. **Interpretation is the least-occupied layer.** No competitor shows fact / legend / fiction labelling. Branching location stories do exist (Story City), but the ones seen are all fiction. No game was found that branches real history by letting a group choose *whose story to follow*. Both points are unresearched, not proven missing. Perspective-choice branching is now an owner-proposed opportunity (OPP-011). *(C-02)*
3. **Help when stuck follows three models**: scarce help, costed help, or one answer only. None is documented for one-time tourists. *(C-03)*
4. **The device model sets price, participation and solo access together.** Examples: a phone per player at a per-person price (Eskap); one phone for about 6 people (Mifrasim); phone packs with shared progress (Questo). *(C-04)*
5. **There are two separate commercial worlds.** Player-paid games sell through marketplaces. Destination-funded games are free to players and paid for by public bodies in France and Spain. The Israeli public-funding side is unresearched. *(C-05)*
6. **Content upkeep decides whether place-based quality lasts.** Changing places break clues. Few competitors show how they test and monitor content. *(C-06)*
7. **The Jerusalem field is thin, Hebrew-first, and groups share one phone.** Whether an English self-guided game in the market is missing is not yet confirmed. *(C-07)*
8. **Food shows up only as a theme, coupon or bundle.** No competitor documents a tasting task with no purchase required. *(C-08)*

## How the pieces fit
DORI's principles (place-based tasks, labelled reveals, self-guided recovery) all raise **content cost per route**. The competitors that scale fastest avoid those costs.

So the **content-operations model and the payer have to fit the educational choices.** The candidates are:
- a per-device consumer price,
- guide or school fees, or
- commissioned routes.

This is the main strategic question to test.

## White spaces
- **Evidence-supported:** only one, and only moderately: a self-guided consumer game with an optional guide layer.
- **Unresearched or not visible without playing:** seven, including perspective-choice branching on real history (WS-10). Don't treat these as market gaps yet.
- **Rejected:** two, because a competitor already does them:
  - solo + family + team in one product (Questo);
  - branching routes in general (Story City).

## Recommended next steps (by information value)
1. **Hotel-room test:** compare remote vs on-site solve rates on a 3-stop paper prototype. Free. *(H-01)*
2. **English catalogue sweep** for Jerusalem market games. 1–2 hours. *(H-10)*
3. **Paid playthroughs** of Mifrasim and Questo Jaffa. About ₪200 + €23. *(H-03)*
4. **Help-ladder A/B test** and **paired-phone test** with families. *(H-05, H-07)*
5. **Branching prototype:** compare linear vs fiction branching vs perspective branching on paper, with 5 groups each. *(H-12)*
