# DORI — Can Cities and Public Bodies Buy City Games?

**Project notes · Research summary · 5 November 2026**  
**Based on:** `DORI_B2G_Municipal_Research_Results.md` and `DORI_B2G_Addendum_Buyers_Terra_Mifrasim.md`  
**Status:** Research findings and ideas to test — **not approved business decisions**.

## The short answer

**Yes, cities and public bodies use city games.** In Israel, the clearest examples are short events for families, residents, and schools. We did **not** find proof that an Israeli city pays a yearly fee for an always-available digital quest. That does **not** mean such contracts do not exist: the research could not access all purchasing records.

Outside Israel, there are stronger examples of public funding, including free games for residents and tourists, one-time setup fees, and yearly upkeep payments.

**For DORI:** It is worth testing public and institutional buyers, but the first offer may work better as a **small, time-limited event or commissioned route** than as a large annual software contract.

## 1. What we found in Israel

| Example | What happened | What it tells us |
|---|---|---|
| [Karmiel](https://tickchak.co.il/16897) (2021) | Municipality organized an urban navigation game for families. | A city can promote a game as a local event; supplier fee is unknown. |
| [Binyamin tourism](https://www.inn.co.il/news/685388) (2025) | A three-day holiday navigation game. Residents paid ₪40 per vehicle; others ₪60. | Holiday games and resident discounts are possible. |
| [Holon](https://www.be106.net/87/58033) (2022) | An outdoor quest for immigrant families, organized through the absorption department. | A game can help new residents get to know their city. This example was not digital. |
| [Israel Nature and Parks Authority](https://www.parks.org.il/new/%D7%9E%D7%A9%D7%97%D7%A7%D7%99-%D7%91%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%97%D7%94-%D7%91%D7%90%D7%AA%D7%A8%D7%99-%D7%A8%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%98%D7%91%D7%A2-%D7%95%D7%94%D7%92%D7%A0%D7%99%D7%9D/) (2020) | Escape and puzzle activities in parks, some free with entry or membership. | Public attractions already offer playful learning. |
| [KKL-JNF tender](https://www.kkl.org.il/files/bid-373-22accessible.pdf) (2022) | A formal tender for a navigation-game app and multiple activities. | Large contracts exist, but supplier requirements can be too demanding for a new company. |
| [CloseApp / Gefen](https://closeapp.co.il/%D7%A4%D7%A2%D7%99%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%99%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%9E%D7%90%D7%95%D7%A9%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%91%D7%92%D7%A4%D7%9F-%D7%94%D7%A4%D7%A2%D7%99%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%99%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%A9%D7%9C%D7%A0%D7%95/) | School navigation activities can be bought using education budgets. | Schools may be a different buyer from city tourism departments. |

**What remains unknown:** Who built most games, what public bodies paid suppliers, and whether any annual contracts were renewed.

## 2. What other countries teach us

- **[Bellevue, USA](https://bellevuewa.gov/city-news/second-scavenger-hunt)** and **[Niagara-on-the-Lake, Canada](https://www.notl.com/node/2621)** ran free public scavenger hunts and repeated them in later years. A repeat event does not prove a yearly software contract.
- **[Barcelona](https://ajuntament.barcelona.cat/turisme/en/noticia/les-gimcanes-digitals-gratuites-descobreixen-7-barris-de-barcelona-2_1286841)** supported free digital games in seven neighborhoods through a grant-backed project. This is **funding a provider**, not necessarily buying a game from it.
- **[South Downs National Park, UK](https://www.southdowns.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/Geotour_Case-Study_June-2016.pdf)** reported £6,100 to set up a GeoTour and £1,250 a year for hosting (2015–16 figures).
- **[Spanish public contract records](https://contratos.gobierto.es/adjudicatarios/m-amp-r-project-management-solutions-s-l)** show small payments such as €99 for renewal/maintenance and €1,383 for promotion (2026). These are different services and **not general price benchmarks for a new game**.
- **[Tèrra Aventura, France](https://www.terra-aventura.fr/sites/default/files/pdf/Dossier%20Presse%20T%C3%A8rra%20Aventura%202025.pdf)** is a free regional geocaching network with millions of reported players. Its regional funding model is much larger than an individual town game.

### Tèrra Aventura: a useful business example

Tèrra Aventura divides work between a regional operator, tourism agencies, local tourism offices and towns. Local partners help with route safety, physical clues and upkeep. Published agreements provide unusually clear prices:

| Offer | One-time creation | Yearly fee |
|---|---:|---:|
| [Standard route, 2021 agreement](https://www.angely.net/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/20210527-D4-Annexe-Creation-dun-parcours-Terra-Aventura-Convention-de-partenariat.pdf) | €1,650 + €125 travel, before tax | €200 before tax |
| [Two-day Micro Z'Aventure, 2024 agreement](https://agglo2b.fr/actes_administratifs//2024/deliberation/D%C3%A9lib%C3%A9ration%20Office%20Tourisme%202024-026%20Cr%C3%A9ation%20Micro%20Z%20aventure%20Terra%20aventura%20convention%20avec%20R%C3%A9gion%20Nouvelle%20Aquitaine.pdf) | €20,710 before tax | €1,200 before tax |

**Lesson to explore:** A buyer may pay for making a route and then pay a smaller fee to keep it working. But these prices come from particular French agreements; they are **not DORI price recommendations**. A place-based DORI route may need more research and editorial upkeep.

## 3. Who might pay for DORI?

These are **buyer hypotheses**, not confirmed leads.

| Possible buyer or channel | What DORI could offer | What must be checked |
|---|---|---|
| **Gap-year and Israel programs** | Self-guided orientation and learning experiences for each new cohort. | Do directors buy these activities? Budget, safety rules, supervision, annual demand. [Example program](https://www.masaisrael.org/program/young-judaea-israel-year-course). |
| **Municipal events and culture teams** | A holiday, neighborhood or community-history quest. | Who approves it, what they pay, whether the event repeats. [Binyamin example](https://www.inn.co.il/news/685388). |
| **Absorption departments** | A welcome-to-the-city quest in suitable languages. | Audience needs, funding and accessible routes. [Holon example](https://www.be106.net/87/58033). |
| **Tourism organizations / JDA** | A visitor experience, voucher offer or partner route. | Eligibility, buyer terms and discount economics. [Jerusalem 2026 supplier call](https://www.itraveljerusalem.com/he/article/suppliers-summer-2026). |
| **Schools and guides** | A route with group controls and educational goals. | Curriculum fit, staff oversight, procurement and pricing. [Gefen example](https://closeapp.co.il/%D7%A4%D7%A2%D7%99%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%99%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%9E%D7%90%D7%95%D7%A9%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%91%D7%92%D7%A4%D7%9F-%D7%94%D7%A4%D7%A2%D7%99%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%99%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%A9%D7%9C%D7%A0%D7%95/). |
| **Marketplaces** | Sell existing DORI experiences to visitors. | Commissions and customer acquisition cost. These are **sales channels, not buyers funding content**. |
| **Parks and heritage sites** | A route for a particular site. | Permission to sell or operate inside the site and competition with existing activities. [Parks authority](https://www.parks.org.il/article/filme/). |
| **Local business groups / sponsors** | A resident or shopping-street edition. | Whether merchants or city business teams see value in it. [Barcelona grant example](https://www.barcelonactiva.cat/-/impulsem-subvenciona-127-projectes). |

### A special case: Jerusalem visitor vouchers

A [summer 2026 iTravelJerusalem supplier program](https://www.itraveljerusalem.com/he/article/suppliers-summer-2026) offered hotel guests a ₪100 digital wallet. The source says experience apps could qualify, with a 25% supplier discount and other terms. This is a way to help customers **buy** a game, not proof the public body will commission its content. DORI's eligibility and participation in a future campaign are **unconfirmed**.

## 4. What about existing Jerusalem competitors?

**[Mifrasim](https://www.mifrasim.org/jerusalem)** offers self-guided Jerusalem games and team activities. Its public pages do not identify a municipal funder. The idea that it benefits from Jerusalem voucher campaigns is **only a hypothesis**.

**[Israel ScaVentures](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g293983-d6201034-Reviews-Israel_ScaVentures-Jerusalem_Jerusalem_District.html)** previously offered games in Mahane Yehuda and Nachlaot. Earlier coverage describes its [scavenger-hunt model](https://jewishlink.news/scaventures-israel-s-latest-touring-trend/). The research found that old product links now return 404 and recent public activity is unclear. **Do not assume the company has closed or that the English-language market is empty.** Verify directly.

## 5. Business choices that matter

1. **Who pays?** A visitor, a school, a tour operator, a municipality or a sponsor may pay for different reasons.
2. **What are they buying?** A one-day event, one route, a package for a group, or an ongoing service.
3. **Who owns the content?** Some public contracts give ownership of new work to the buyer. DORI must distinguish its platform from commissioned content. See the [KKL tender](https://www.kkl.org.il/files/bid-373-22accessible.pdf).
4. **Who keeps the route accurate?** Shops close, signs change, and physical clues disappear. Maintenance needs a clear owner and budget.
5. **What happens to Amazing Shuk sales?** A free public edition could compete with the paid tourist version. A separate resident event or a different neighborhood may reduce that risk.
6. **What legal and access work is needed?** Accessibility, privacy, purchasing rules, and site permits must be checked for each deal. See [accessibility guidance](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%94%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%A9%D7%AA_%D7%90%D7%AA%D7%A8%D7%99_%D7%90%D7%99%D7%A0%D7%98%D7%A8%D7%A0%D7%98_%D7%95%D7%90%D7%A4%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%A7%D7%A6%D7%99%D7%95%D7%AA_%D7%9C%D7%90%D7%A0%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%9D_%D7%A2%D7%9D_%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%92%D7%91%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%AA), [privacy law overview](https://www.chamber.org.il/serviceslobby/legal/74023/164240/) and [local-purchase thresholds (2025)](https://www.wdzf.co.il/tenderssums/). **Do not rely on the research for current legal thresholds or tender eligibility.**

## 6. Recommended next research — not yet approved

**First, speak to potential buyers before building a separate B2G product.** The following is a proposed research order:

1. **Five gap-year program directors:** Would a self-guided educational game help their groups? What would they pay? Who handles safety?
2. **Two or three municipal departments:** Would they fund a holiday or neighborhood quest? Who holds the budget? Would they buy again next year?
3. **Jerusalem tourism / voucher managers:** Can DORI join a future campaign? What are the real discounts, commissions and eligibility rules?
4. **Mifrasim and Israel ScaVentures:** Confirm what they currently sell, for whom, and how their games are used.
5. **A procurement or legal adviser:** Check the applicable purchasing process, content ownership, accessibility and permissions before making a formal offer.

For each interview, record the buyer's problem, target audience, expected results, budget, buying process, decision-maker, and what proof they would need before paying.

## 7. Current conclusion and evidence limits

**Working view:** Small, event-based public projects appear more plausible as a first Israeli B2G offer than a multi-year city-game licence. Gap-year programs and education providers are promising **B2B hypotheses**, while marketplaces and vouchers are **distribution channels** rather than content buyers.

**Not established:** Israeli municipal contract prices, demand for DORI's format, supplier renewals, future funding availability, DORI's legal eligibility, or visitor willingness to pay. The French and Spanish examples show possible models, **not proven Israeli demand**.

This summary uses research collected on **5 November 2026**. It is a simplified project reference, not new independent verification. Keep the two original research files for full sources, contract details and contradictions.
