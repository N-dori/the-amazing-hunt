# DORI B2G Research Results — Selling Quests to Municipalities and Regional Councils

**Executes:** H-08 (C-05) · **Prompt:** `claude/DORI_B2G_Municipal_Research_Prompt.md`
**Run:** 5 Nov 2026, web search (Hebrew, English, Spanish, French, German, Dutch). All sources read on 2026-11-05 unless a source date is given.
**Labels:** OBS = a source states it · INF = my reasoning · UNK = not found · NO = a source confirms absence · NOT PUBLIC = exists but undisclosed.
**Method limits:** No access to municipal procurement-committee databases, Open Budget (obudget) contract search, or paywalled tender aggregators (Wizbiz, Michraziya). Several government pages failed to load (Innovation Authority pilot tracks). Negative findings below are "not found with public web search", not "confirmed absent".

---

## 1. Bottom line

- **An Israeli precedent exists, but only as events and programmes, not as licensed or commissioned always-on quests.** Found: a Karmiel Municipality urban navigation game (2021), a Binyamin regional tourism Hanukkah navigation game with a resident discount (2025), a Holon absorption-department quest for immigrant families (2022), and a 2015 free Jerusalem audio-tour app co-funded by the JDA, the municipality and a ministry. OBS.
- **No Israeli case found of a public body paying a yearly licence or retainer for a city quest.** No contract value is public for any Israeli case. UNK / NOT PUBLIC.
- **Most likely buying route: small exempt purchases.** Below the exemption threshold (NIS 164,500 incl. VAT for municipalities; NIS 81,100 for councils up to 20k residents, index 3/2025), no tender record is published, which explains why nothing surfaces. Regulation 3(5) (artistic or literary work) is a further exemption that fits authored quest content. OBS for the thresholds; INF for the fit.
- **The one large quasi-public tender (KKL-JNF 373/22, 2022) shows what a full B2G contract looks like** (24 months plus 3×12 options, price 60 / quality 40, IP of deliverables to the buyer, strict SLAs). DORI would not qualify: it required an app live for 3+ years and 10+ organisational clients. OBS.
- **Internationally the model is well documented and cheap per unit.** Spanish councils pay €99 to €1,383 per year for gymkana upkeep or promotion. A UK national park paid £6,100 setup plus £1,250 per year for a GeoTour. Nouvelle-Aquitaine spends €950k per year on Tèrra Aventura for 3M+ players. OBS.
- **Resident-focused public quests are mostly free, seasonal and event-shaped** (Bellevue, Niagara-on-the-Lake: GooseChase hunts run by the city, repeated a second year). Resident discounts on paid games also exist (Binyamin: NIS 40 residents vs NIS 60). OBS.
- **Verdict:** Hypothesis (b), "they buy events, not digital quests", is best supported. The channel is real but small-ticket and event-driven. A retainer or licence model is unproven in Israel. **Test first:** a time-boxed, event-shaped resident quest (holiday, neighbourhood anniversary, or immigrant welcome) sold to a regional-council tourism unit or a municipal absorption department, priced well under the exemption threshold. INF.
- **Evidence strength:** Israel = weak (single-source press items, no contract records). International = moderate to strong (contract records in Spain, published budgets in France and the UK).

## 4. What resident-focused quests look like (vs tourist quests)

| Dimension | Resident-focused (observed) | Tourist-focused (observed, earlier phases) |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Community bonding and getting families outdoors (Bellevue, NOTL); immigrant integration (Holon); local heritage and identity (Binyamin, kibbutz anniversaries) | Visitor spread, overnight stays, spend (Tèrra: 38% overnight; €35M direct spend claimed) |
| Time shape | **Time-boxed**: 3 days (Binyamin), 4 weeks (Bellevue), 6 weeks (NOTL), a single day (Karmiel, Holon) | Always-on, 24/7 (Barcelona, Tèrra) |
| Trigger | Holidays (Hanukkah), seasons (summer, spring), anniversaries, welcome programmes | Trip planning |
| Content | Parks, neighbourhood, local history, civic themes (recycling, pets) | Landmarks, legends |
| Social layer | Prizes, raffles, city merchandise, end-of-game party, social hashtags | Scores, rarely social |
| Languages | Local language plus the immigrant language (Holon: Russian) | Multilingual (Barcelona: 4) |
| Promotion | City news releases and municipal channels | OTAs, tourism portals |

INF: a resident quest is closer to a **municipal event product** than a content licence. That pushes pricing toward per-edition fees, not yearly licences.

## 10. Risks and conflicts

- **Cannibalisation (INF).** If a public body funds Amazing Shuk content and makes it free, paid B2C sales in Mahane Yehuda collapse. The evidence offers three mitigations: (1) resident-only pricing or codes (Binyamin), (2) time-boxed editions (Bellevue, NOTL), (3) commissioned routes in **other** neighbourhoods or towns, keeping the flagship paid.
- **Event-fee ceiling (INF).** Event budgets are small and annual. Without a renewal pattern (only Karmiel hints at one), revenue is lumpy.
- **Compliance cost (OBS → INF).** IS 5568 accessibility, an accessibility statement, Amendment 13 privacy obligations and DPO-facing paperwork apply from the first public contract. That is fixed overhead for a small vendor.
- **IP leakage (OBS).** Buyer-owned deliverables (KKL model) may block reuse of authored content across clients. Negotiate platform/content separation and a reuse licence.
- **Eligibility gap (OBS).** Formal tenders require multi-year track records and client counts. DORI must start with exempt small purchases.
- **Political and budget timing (INF).** Ministry of Tourism grants closed for 2026. The Gefen pool is closed. The next windows are 2027.
- **Content drift (from C-05/C-06).** Promotional demands from funders may push toward generic and promotional content and away from DORI's fact/legend/interpretation labelling.
