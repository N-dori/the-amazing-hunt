## Summary

1. **The reveal's chain mostly holds, with one correction.** Genesis 49:9, Judah as "a lion's whelp", Esther 2:5's Benjaminite Mordecai called *ish yehudi*, and *Yehudi* → *Ioudaios* → *Iudaeus* → Old French *giu* → *Jew* are all sourced (S1, S3). **But *Yehud* is not a step between Judah and *Yehudi*.** The word *yehudim* already appears for the people of the kingdom of Judah in 2 Kings 16:6, before the exile, centuries before the Persian province of Yehud (S1, S3). Write it as Judah → *Yehudi* (a man of Judah), with Yehud as a side note at most.
2. **Dan is also "a lion's whelp"** (Deut. 33:22, *Dan gur aryeh*), in the same words as Judah's *gur aryeh Yehuda* (S1). This confirms the plan's rule: never make Dan an option, and never write a question whose only clue is "the tribe called a lion".
3. **The Lions' Gate animals:** sources agree they are better read as **leopards or panthers, the emblem of the Mamluk sultan Baybars**. Moshe Sharon matches them to Baybars' felines at Jisr Jindas and Qasr al-Basha, dated about 1273. The gate itself is Suleiman's, about 1538 (S4, S5). The tale that Selim dreamed of lions, and that this made the lion Jerusalem's symbol, is a **legend**, flagged as dubious (S4). This agrees with the stamp flyer in run 07 (S1 there): Baybars' leopard, later adopted by Jerusalem's Jews as the Lion of Judah.
4. **Jerusalem's emblem:** the city has used a lion on a shield since 1949. The committee's own reading: the lion of the tribe of Judah (*gur aryeh Yehuda*), later the symbol of the kingdom of Judah, whose capital was Jerusalem (S6).
5. **The market's name does not come from the tribe.** Mahane Yehuda (founded 1887 by Frutiger, Konstrum and Yosef Navon) was named after **Yehuda Navon, Yosef Navon's brother, who died young**; the market took its name from the neighbourhood (S7, S8). So no market-name link to the lion should be made. A safe, true line instead: the market is named after a man called Yehuda, and his name is Judah's name (INFERRED; not a source claim).
6. **The gate's full Hebrew inscription** is transcribed in Hebrew Wikipedia (S2): it begins **אבני זיכרון מזכרת לכבוד ולתפארת לזכר עולם יהיה צדיק…**, above the neighbourhood's entrance from Agripas St, with the English line "Sir Moses Montifiori Testimonial".
7. **"Stones of remembrance" is a biblical phrase.** *Avnei zikkaron* is exactly Exodus 28:12 and 39:7: the two stones on the high priest's shoulder-pieces, engraved with the tribes' names, carried "as stones for remembrance of the Israelite people" (S1). The gate's next words, *le-zekher olam yihyeh tzaddik* ("the righteous will be remembered forever"), quote Psalm 112:6 (S1). So the plan's "to be confirmed in research" is confirmed for the phrase; that the gate's author *meant* the ephod is an interpretation.

### Interpretation (reasoned reading)

- The gate's builders chose a phrase from Exodus for a dedication stone: the stones at the gate "remember" Montefiore as the ephod's stones "remembered" the tribes. The text never names Exodus; this is a reading, to be phrased "The words echo…".
- The Hebrew inscription is rhymed: its lines end in *-ret* (הכותרת, מסותרת, מעוטרת, מאושרת, עשרת), the style of 19th-century Jerusalem dedications. Read from S2's text; not a source claim.
- *Yehuda* as a first name (Yehuda Navon) is Judah's name, so the market carries the tribe's name through a person, not through a choice to honour the tribe.

### Attributed tradition

- Selim I's dream of lions, and the lion becoming Jerusalem's symbol because of it (S4, flagged dubious; S5).
- The Lions' Gate carvings commemorating the Ottoman victory over the Mamluks in 1517 (S4, S5). Sharon's dating to Baybars (about 1273) points to reused Mamluk carvings instead; the sources differ.

## The inscription (S2, as transcribed; not yet checked against the stone)

> אבני זיכרון מזכרת לכבוד ולתפארת לזכר עולם יהיה צדיק גולת הכותרת. השר הגדול סיר משה מונטיפיורי זלה"ה אוהב את התורה מאהבה מסותרת תנצב"א מעוטרת אמן. ואנחנו נברך רב ברכות לכבוד השרים והסגנים גבאי החברה מזכרת משה בק"ק לונדון יע"א למשמרת יבלו ימיהם בטוב ושנותיהם בנעימים ברוב עושר וכבוד והצלחה מאושרת ושכרם כפול מאת ד' על הד מונים עשרת אמן

A working gloss (not approved copy): "Stones of remembrance, a memorial for honour and glory. 'The righteous will be remembered forever' (Ps. 112:6), the crown of all: the great prince Sir Moses Montefiore, of blessed memory, who loved the Torah with a hidden love… And we bless the officers of the Mazkeret Moshe society of the holy community of London…"

S2 writes זיכרון (full spelling); the owner's photo reads זכרון. The stone decides.

## What this means for the two challenges

### "A Hidden Symbol" (the mural's lion riddle)

- **Step 1 (find the lion):** still rests on run 07's S1 (the Israel Post flyer) alone. Needs the field check before build.
- **Step 2 (יהודה → יהודי):** the letter change is sound (one letter, ה → י). It needs a real sign with יהודה in sight.
- **The reveal, corrected chain:** Gen. 49:9, Judah the lion's whelp → the tribe, then the kingdom of Judah (its people were already *yehudim*, 2 Kings 16:6) → after the kingdom, *Yehudi* meant the whole people: Mordecai the Benjaminite is *ish yehudi* (Esther 2:5) → *Ioudaios* → *Iudaeus* → *giu* → *Jew*. Drop Yehud from the main line, or keep it as "under the Persians the land itself was called Yehud".
- **The twist:** the Lions' Gate "lions" are probably Baybars' leopards, a Muslim sultan's emblem, which Jerusalem's Jews later read as the Lion of Judah (run 07 S1; S4). Phrase it with a hedge ("Historians think…").
- **The market's name:** don't link it to the lion. If used at all: "The market is named after a man called Yehuda Navon. His name is Judah's name too."
- **Never a claim about anyone's ancestry** (unchanged from the plan).

### "Say it like Ohel Moshe" (*avnei zikaron*)

- **The phrase is confirmed** as biblical (Ex. 28:12; 39:7) and is the first words of the gate's Hebrew text (S2).
- **Transliteration:** modern Israeli *avnei zikaron* (av-NEI zi-ka-RON); biblical *avnei zikkaron*, with a doubled *k* (S1's vowel marks). Use the modern one in the app; the clip decides.
- **Reveal material (drafts need approval):** the ephod's two stones carried the twelve tribes' names "for remembrance"; this gate's stones carry Montefiore's name; the next line quotes Psalm 112:6.
