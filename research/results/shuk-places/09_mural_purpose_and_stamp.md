## Summary

1. **The Jerusalem purpose is sourced: tourism.** CitéCréation's own page for the "Painted mural circuit JERUSALEM" (8 murals over 15 years) asks: "How is it possible to attract tourists when political tensions in this part of the world make them think twice about coming?" East Jerusalem Development, the Ministry of Tourism and the City of Jerusalem asked for the circuit (S1). Run 07's flyer adds that the area had been under renewal since 1987 (run 07 S1).
2. **Their general approach is sourced: murals in ordinary and social-housing districts, made with the residents.** In Lyon, the Tony Garnier Urban Museum is 25 murals on the gable walls of an inter-war social-housing district (S3). A Taylor & Francis chapter describes CitéCréation working in "priority neighbourhoods" alongside French urban renewal programmes (S4). CitéCréation's Halim Bensaïd: "It completely transformed the quartier… It has given something back to the residents: pride" (S3, quoted; a CitéCréation figure, not an independent evaluation).
3. **The real-estate claim is not sourced.** No study was found that measures a CitéCréation mural's effect on property prices, in Jerusalem or anywhere. The pages that claim murals raise prices cite no study. One gives an unsourced "10 %"; another still has template markers in its text (S5). Croix-Rousse in Lyon is now expensive, but the source doesn't attribute that to its mural (S6). **UNKNOWN**: it can't be an answer or a fact in the game yet.
4. **It is a real stamp, and its face value is ₪15.** Issue 1171 (September 2024) is a real Israel Post stamp (run 07 S1). The Stampworld catalogue lists "Street Art – The Market", September 2024, with a face value of **15 NIS** (S7; a catalogue, to be confirmed on Israel Post's issue page). The shop sells a **souvenir sheet** ("גיליונית מזכרת – אומנות קיר השוק, ירושלים") for **₪20.00**, and its description says the full sheet holds 15 stamps (S2). So the page has two 15s and a 20.
5. **The technique: the sources differ on the surface.** The flyer calls it trompe-l'œil on a bare concrete wall (run 07 S1). CitéCréation's page names only "coated concrete" and doesn't say trompe-l'œil (S1). Rafi Kfir says canvas glued to the wall (handoff, 24 Oct 2026). "Trompe-l'œil" itself is safe (run 07 S1); the surface isn't.
6. **Who painted it:** CitéCréation's page names no individual artists, only "CitéCréation's mural painters" (S1). Its gallery file name for this mural is Agripas "Le Marché".
