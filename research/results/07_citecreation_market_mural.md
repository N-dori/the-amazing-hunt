## Summary

1. **Year: Hanukkah 2001.** The official Israel Post stamp flyer says so (S1). That settles "2001 vs 2007": the 2007 lead in `Mission_Task_Ideas.md` most likely refers to another CitéCréation mural in Jerusalem (see "Other CitéCréation work" below).
2. **Artists: CitéCréation**, a mural cooperative from Oullins near Lyon (in Hebrew "עיר היצירה"). According to a 2015 article, 12 of its artists painted it (S1, S2).
3. **"103" is not the street number.** The stamp flyer lists the "103 call center" (מוקד 103) among the mural's details. 103 is the Israel Electric Corporation's (IEC) fault line, so the "103 tile" seen in photo #104 is very likely part of the painting, not a house number. That undermines the "Agrippas St 103" lead in the ideas file.
4. **Location:** Agripas St, on the west side of a residential block built 1935–1937, where Beit Ya'akov St meets Agripas St (S1, S2). Run 06's "70 Agripas St" fits better than "103", but the house number is still `[TO VERIFY]` on site.
5. **The mural shows real, named people** from the market (S1). For the game, these count as painted portraits of real people under A8: crop objects only.
6. **Rights holder: CitéCréation** (© CitéCréation, contact@citecreation.fr). Permission for crops and the altered "what's missing?" image goes to them (R-01).
7. **The mural was on an Israeli stamp in September 2024**: Israel Post issue 1171, "Murals – The Market, Jerusalem" (S1).

### Interpretation

- **"103" is the IEC fault line, not the address.** It's reasoned from S1's detail list plus the IEC generator-room story. Confirm on site that the 103 in #104 is painted.
- **"Real or painted?" now has documented answers.** The blinds and railings are real, but they were repainted to suit the mural. The IEC workers and the "danger, high voltage" door are painted, and they sit on a real generator room. The falafel shop below is real. These are candidates for the opening task's answer key; each needs checking against the wall.
- **"Date the painting":** 2001 fits the clue in the ideas file (a man on a phone with an antenna).
- **The "real people in the painting" angle fits the game's premise.** Residents asked to see their own market, and some of them are in it.

### Attributed tradition

- Drivers stuck in traffic on Agripas can't tell the painted balconies from the real ones (S2, the writers' account).

### Not found or unresolved

## For rights (R-01, A5)

- **Rights holder:** CitéCréation; the site footer reads "© CitéCréation". Contact: contact@citecreation.fr, +33 4 78 50 44 57, citecreation.fr/nous-contacter/.
- **What to ask:**
  - permission to use crops of the Agripas mural from DORI's own photos, in the game;
  - permission to show a digitally altered version (the "what's missing?" round, labelled Fiction);
  - the credit line they want;
  - the artists' names.
- **The people in the mural** are real, named residents. Even with CitéCréation's permission, crop objects only, unless the people or their families agree (A8).
- **Stamp:** the 2024 Israel Post stamp is a separate work by Pini Hamou. Don't reproduce it without Israel Post's permission.
