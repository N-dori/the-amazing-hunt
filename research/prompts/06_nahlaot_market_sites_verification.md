# ROLE / DECISION

Act as a historian-researcher of Jerusalem working in English and Hebrew. Your research decides **which planned stops in Nahlaot and Mahane Yehuda are documented well enough to build a game mission on**, where exactly each one is, and which stories must stay labelled as "attributed local tradition".

# RESEARCH QUESTION

For each stop below, establish: (a) whether the place or story is documented, and by whom; (b) its exact location (street address, or a description precise enough to find on foot) and whether it is publicly visible or accessible; (c) the core facts that reliable sources support, and the common myths or contested versions; (d) available historical images, with archive, catalog ID and rights status; (e) for businesses, whether there is evidence the business is still operating in 2026.

Search in **both Hebrew and English**. Hebrew names are given as search hints.

**Nahlaot (נחלאות)**
1. Rabbi Aryeh Levin's home (רבי אריה לוין) and the memorial to Rabbi Shalom Haim Tzarum
2. Batei Goral (בתי גורל): the lottery story behind the name, and the Yemenite community. Also an 1882 *Havatzelet* report of about 200 destitute Yemenite Jews arriving in Jerusalem: is it real, and does it connect to Nahlaot?
3. Ohel Moshe (אהל משה): the synagogue and its communal oven, founding date, and Montefiore's role; any documented inscription or plaque naming Montefiore in Ohel Moshe or Mazkeret Moshe
4. The Navon family home and the "Picture in Stone" (תמונה באבן) project: what it is, who made it, where its images are
5. Gan HaTut (גן התות, the Mulberry Garden) and its water cisterns: is any cistern feature visible today? Also a Yossi Banai memorial plaque reportedly nearby, and the Ohavei Zion synagogue his family helped found (1905?)
6. Bustan Sephardi garden (בוסתן ספרדי) and Ladino
7. Hesed VeRahamim synagogue (חסד ורחמים), reportedly at 18 Carmel Street: a former drinking establishment that became a synagogue (1925?)
8. A Kurdish community mosaic reportedly at the end of HaYarkon Street

**Mahane Yehuda (שוק מחנה יהודה)**
9. The market's origins: the Valero family land, the name "Mahane Yehuda", and the "Loan and Savings" (מחנה יהודה / "חסכון והלוואה") section name
10. Rami Levy's first shop, reportedly at 4 HaShikma Street in 1976. Rely on his own interviews (e.g. Globes) and independent reporting. Also find balanced, sourced analysis of large retail buyers' effect on Israeli farmers and prices. **Do not attribute specific contracts or market-wide price effects to Rami Levy without a source.**
11. The 1980s market renewal (stone paving, roof, first elected traders' committee) and community worker Uri Amadi
12. The Mahane Yehuda market mural
13. Rahamim's produce stall (a stall listed at 22 Mahane Yehuda Street?) and charity stories: what is documented vs anecdote
14. Food stops: Marzipan bakery and its rugelach (44 Agripas Street; is it still open?), Rahmo (5 HaEshkol Street), malawach and Yemenite food in the market, Azura, Hayazdi spices, and tahini ground with millstones. Also the documented history of rugelach and bourekas in Jerusalem. Do not repeat unsupported claims (e.g. rugelach as a "baklava-croissant fusion", or health effects of etrog drinks).
15. The 12 April 2002 attack at the market: basic verified facts only, so a guide can mention it with care

# OUTPUT

**Rules specific to this task:** never fill a gap with a plausible-sounding story. A tourism blog or tour-business site alone is not verification; say so when it is the only source. Separate novels, TV series and folklore from documented history. Cite Hebrew sources with the Hebrew title and a short English gloss.

**Source priorities:** National Library of Israel and its blog, Yad Ben-Zvi, Jerusalem Municipality, Israel State Archives, academic works on Jerusalem neighbourhoods, reputable Israeli press (Haaretz, Ynet, Globes, Times of Israel, Jerusalem Post), and Hebrew Wikipedia only as a lead to primary sources.
