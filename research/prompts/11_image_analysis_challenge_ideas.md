# Research prompt 11: challenge ideas where image analysis adds something

Written 3 Nov 2026 for Claude on the web (web search on). Short run. The answer goes to `research/results/11_image_analysis_challenge_ideas.md`. Follows [run 10](../results/10_photo_answer_verification.md) (how to verify a photo).

---

**Context.** An outdoor walking game for families and visitors in Jerusalem's Nahlaot and Mahane Yehuda market (mobile web, small groups, a guide nearby). The game's location check is GPS. At some stops a group can answer with a photo, and a vision model or an in-house image model checks it; a typed answer is always the fallback.

**Find:** challenge mechanics, from real games, apps, museums, tourism and education, where analysing a photo makes something possible that a typed answer or a fixed reference photo can't. Examples of what we mean:
- "find any X" hunts (any carved stone, any lion);
- details high up or hard to see, where the zoomed photo becomes the way to look;
- recreating an archival photo or a mural's pose;
- creative group poses (e.g. framing someone you love inside a heart made with your hands);
- counting, colour or spot-the-difference against a historical image;
- street art and graffiti.

**For each idea:**
- what the players do;
- who uses it (product or study, with a link and date);
- what the model must judge;
- known failure modes;
- privacy risk (bystanders, children in the frame).

Look at: Google Lens and Gemini scavenger hunts, Goosechase, Actionbound, Scavify, Museum Hack, Smartify, Pokémon GO AR photo tasks, Snapchat Lenses, "AI photo scavenger hunt" apps (2023–2026), and education research on photo-based place learning.

**Output:**
1. 15–25 ideas in a table, best first.
2. The 5 that best suit a family walk in an old neighbourhood and a busy market.
3. Ideas to avoid, and why.
4. A source register (publisher · date · URL).

Label each claim OBSERVED, INFERRED or UNKNOWN. Don't ask clarifying questions; state your assumptions and proceed.
