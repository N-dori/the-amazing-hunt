# The market mural: content rework (draft for the owner, 4 Nov 2026)

**Status:** content only, awaiting the owner's approval. Nothing here is built. Every visitor-facing line is a draft (`TODO(copy)`).

**How it was made:** the owner's order for the stop (4 Nov), worked through `/experience-design` at medium depth. No external research on precedents; the facts come from `docs/knowledge/market-mural-agrippas.md` (M-sources) and [run 12](../../research/results/shuk-places/12_mural_rework_checks.md).

**Answer forms vary** (top-level rule, 3 Nov): typed, choose, a typed number, choose at the wall, and talk. No photo in this round.

**Length: about 20–25 minutes** (the old stop was about 8). That's long for the opening stop in the sun. **Owner's call:** keep all four as Main, or mark parts 2 and 3 **Optional** (plan §6 labels), so a tired group can go from 1 to 4 to the AHA.

## 1. Who painted it? · Easy

- **What they do:** walk along the wall and find the plaque. It's low and easy to reach, so a good job for a child. The Answer Submitter types the name.
- **Question:**
  > `TODO(copy)`: Somewhere on this wall, the painters left their name. Find it. Who painted the market?
- **Answer key:** CitéCréation (M1, M2).
  - **Accept:** "CitéCréation", "Cite Creation", "citecreation", "Cité-Création" and "עיר היצירה".
  - **Normalise:** case, accents, spaces and hyphens.
  - **Never:** a person's name. CitéCréation names no individual artists (M3).
- **Hints:**
  1. `TODO(copy)`: Look low, beside the painted shop, near a small yellow warning sign.
  2. `TODO(copy)`: It's a French name: two words joined, "city" and "creation".
- **Reveal** (`TODO(copy)`):
  > CitéCréation is a cooperative of mural painters from near Lyon, France. Twelve of its artists painted this wall, and it was unveiled at Hanukkah 2001. Their own page names no single painter, only the team.
  - **Facts:** M1, M2 and M3, all Historical fact.
- **Depends on field check 1:**
  - The plaque is left of the shop, under the warning triangle, and it is already in the artists' photo (run 12).
  - **What it says is unknown.** If it doesn't name CitéCréation, this challenge needs another source on the wall, or it can't stand as it is.
- **The no-device path:** the guide confirms, or the stuck path ("I'm completely stuck").

## 3. The stamp · Tricky

- **What they do:** the Researcher opens Israel Post's shop page. The page is in Hebrew, but the price needs none.
- **Link:** `https://shop.israelpost.co.il/אומנות-קיר-השוק-ירושלים/`
- **Question:**
  > `TODO(copy)`: This wall is on a real stamp sheet. How much does the souvenir sheet cost, in shekels?
- **Answer key:** **20** (M10, ₪20.00 on 4 Nov 2026).
  - **Accept:** "20", "₪20", "20.00" and "20 shekels"; normalise to the number.
  - **The trap (the owner's 15 / 20 idea):** the page also shows "15 stamps" and "₪99" (free shipping).
- **Hints:**
  1. `TODO(copy)`: Not every number on the page is a price.
  2. `TODO(copy)`: Look for the ₪ sign near the top.
- **Reveal** (`TODO(copy)`):
  > In September 2024, Israel Post put this wall on a stamp: issue 1171, "Murals – The Market, Jerusalem", designed by Pini Hamou. You're standing in front of the real one.
  - **Facts:** M1, Historical fact.
  - **No "15" of any kind in the reveal.** "15 stamps" and "face value 15 NIS" don't fit together (sources differ, run 12) until Israel Post's issue page is read.
- **Risks:**
  - The price can change: check before each deploy. If it changes, the answer key changes with it.
  - The page also names CitéCréation, 2001, "אחיזת עיניים" (trompe-l'œil) and the lion. Part 1 comes before it, so the name is safe. The technique (part 4) could leak, but only in Hebrew text below the price.
  - **Recommendation:** keep the owner's order.
- **The hotel-room test:** this part could be played anywhere. It earns its place by the surprise that this wall is on a stamp, and it's short. Mark it Optional if the stop runs long.

## 5. Why paint a market here? (AHA)

- **The moment:** the answer they reach together is the one they'll take home. Artists came from France to paint the market *with* the people who live beside it, in a place few people looked at.
- **Predict first** (unscored; spoken, nothing typed or stored):
  > `TODO(copy)`: Before you go on: why would a team from France paint a market on a wall in this corner? Say your guesses out loud.
- **Reveal** (`TODO(copy)`, about 55 words):
  > CitéCréation paints where few people look: ordinary streets and social-housing districts, together with the people who live there. Here, the residents chose the subject, the market, and some of them are painted in it. And Jerusalem wanted visitors back, at a time when tensions kept them away.

  | Line | Label | Source |
  |---|---|---|
  | Where CitéCréation paints, and with whom | Historical fact | M6, M7 |
  | The residents chose the subject; some are painted in it | Historical fact | M1 |
  | Jerusalem wanted visitors back | Historical fact | M3 |

- **Optional depth:** "It has given something back to the residents: pride." Halim Bensaïd said this **about a district in Lyon**, not this wall (M6). It can be used only with that said.
- **Never:** that murals raise property values (Unknown, no source). And no names of the painted residents: who they are and their consent are Unknown.
- **Bridge to the market and the whole game** (`TODO(copy)`):
  > Now walk into the real market. Some of what you'll hear today is fact, some is told, and some is painted over. Keep looking.
  - Mission_Task_Ideas' opener hook, reworded.

A solo player holds every role (D12). A group without a Researcher: the leader gives the role to someone; the role can be combined with another.

## Critique (`/experience-design` step 8)

- **Better than the old stop:**
  - one mechanic becomes five forms;
  - the group needs each other in part 2;
  - the empty reveal now carries the stop's meaning: the AHA.
- **Screen time:** parts 2 and 3 put a phone in the Researcher's hand. Part 2 is written as a conversation (one phone, many eyes on the wall) to limit it. Parts 1 and 4a happen at the wall.
- **Quiz after every story:** four scored questions in a row risk it. They're broken up by different forms and by the spoken AHA, which carries no score.
- **Disguised test:** none. No answer is in a text they've just read: the name is on the wall, the price on a page, the real items on the wall, the technique's name after the experience.
- **Hotel-room test:** fails only for part 3 (see above).
- **Length:** see "The stop at a glance"; the owner decides Main or Optional.
- **The lion riddle** stays parked. The tree now hides the area where the keystone was in the artists' photo, which makes field check 1 more doubtful (run 12).
