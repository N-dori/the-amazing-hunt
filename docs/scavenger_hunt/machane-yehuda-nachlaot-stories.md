## 1. Machane Yehuda Market (שוק מחנה יהודה)

### 1.1 Uri Amadi — the man who ran the market from a vegetable crate
*Sources: `אורי עמדי.doc` (article by Shlomo Tzezana, dated 2 Dec 2004) · `סמל וסוס ארוך.doc` (Nachlaot folder)*

- **The setup.** In the 1980s, Uri Amadi, director of the Mekor Baruch community center, saw the neighbourhoods across Jaffa Road — the city center, the market and Nachlaot — falling apart socially and economically. City hall's private talk was about "drying out" the market or relocating it; many saw it as a social sore run by people with criminal records, by their own rules.
- **Teddy Kollek's put-down.** When Amadi pitched the idea, Mayor Teddy Kollek brushed him off as naïve: what can you do with market traders whose only initiative is burning down the clubhouse of whichever team beats Beitar? *(Both docs carry this anecdote, in slightly different wording.)*
- **The "office."** For about six months, unpaid, Amadi sat on a wooden vegetable crate in the middle of the market. *(סמל וסוס ארוך)*
- **The rumours.** Suspicious traders whispered he was from the tax authority; others said the police; cynics assumed he was a small-time politician collecting power in the alleys. *(אורי עמדי)*
- **Football as glue.** He found the lowest common denominator — football — formed teams of stallholders and trained them in Gan Sacher.
- **The Purim "hafla."** Ahead of Purim he got dozens of traders onto two loaded buses to an IDF base where, together with a Kurdish band, they threw a Kurdish party for the soldiers. Support for the IDF turned out to be the widest common ground among the traders.
- **The big cleanup.** After a 5-day strike left the market buried in garbage, Amadi brought the "Ashkenazi kids" from the Experimental School (בית הספר הניסויי) to roll up sleeves alongside the Sephardi traders and clean it up.
- **Life-cycle presence.** He attended traders' family celebrations in the evenings and prayed Shacharit at shiva houses.
- **First-ever census and election.** He ran the first accurate registry of stall owners, shop owners and building owners — **450** in total — which led to the **first secret democratic election in the market's ~100-year history**, producing an elected traders' committee.
- **Three years later**, Uzi Wexler (then the city's strongman) summoned him to lead the market's physical renewal: stone paving instead of asphalt, a roof replacing the leaking tin sheets, lighting, sewage — without killing the market's character. Closing a live market for months was considered impossible; Amadi was the only one the traders would accept it from. His line: you don't just show up one day and close the market — he'd known the people for three years.
- **"Don't burn the clubhouse."** When he was later pushed off the downtown project, market activists offered to burn the club in protest. He refused: no political fight and no violent one — break either rule and he has failed, whatever the outcome.
- **Outcome.** The market experiment became the proof of concept for the "Lev Ha'Ir" (City Heart) project. Amadi was later named one of the most influential figures in Jerusalem's past century and won a prize from a Swedish foundation; among the few guests at the ceremony were two Machane Yehuda traders.
- **Kahane connection (context).** Amadi notes Rabbi Meir Kahane set up his office in Beit Eini near the market, reading the area's social tensions as a recruiting ground. *(אורי עמדי)*

### 1.2 Yossi Banai — a market kid
*Source: photo `20210215_103249.jpg` (memorial plaque, "Jerusalem in Stone" series; shot 15 Feb 2021)*

- Yossi Banai (1932–2006) was born in Zichron Yosef, **grew up in Machane Yehuda**, went to the Alliance school, and as a boy prayed at the **Ohavei Zion synagogue**, which his family helped found in **1905**.
- Actor, director, singer, lyricist, sketch writer — Israel Prize laureate.
- The plaque sits by **Gan HaTut (the Mulberry Garden)**, which references his song *"Ani VeSimon UMoiz HaKatan"* — a nostalgic song about his childhood alleys, the mulberry tree, rooftops, and the long-gone Rex cinema. *(Lyrics not reproduced here.)*

### 1.3 The children's games from Banai's song: "Semel" & "Sus Aroch"
*Source: `סמל וסוס ארוך.doc`*

- **Semel (סְמֶל)** — an old Jerusalem game (known elsewhere as "Asumaniya", "Avudaniya", "Hamor Katzar"…). One kid bends over behind a line; the others leapfrog him shouting *"Semel!"*. After each round he steps further from the line, until someone fails the jump and becomes the new "donkey".
- **Sus Aroch (Long Horse)** — two teams. One team forms a bent-over chain, each head between the legs of the kid in front; the other team jumps onto their backs trying to collapse it. The trick: pile your heaviest jumpers onto the "weak links". Count to ten — if the horse collapses, the jumpers win.
- Great interactive stop for family groups.

## 2. Nachlaot (נחלאות)

### 2.1 Neighbourhood facts
*Source: `אורי עמדי.doc`*

- As of the 2004 article: ~15,000 residents in Jerusalem's defined city center, **~6,000 of them in Nachlaot**.
- **Beit Wiener in Mazkeret Moshe** (part of the Nachlaot cluster) received a $0.5M donation for renovation; Amadi hosted groups there from Israel and abroad.

### 2.2 The Kurdish mosaic
*Source: `סמל וסוס ארוך.doc`*

- At the end of **HaYarkon Street**, past two synagogues (named in the doc as Barazani [spelled "בראשי"] and Amadi), turn left to a several-metre mosaic telling the story of the **Kurdish community in Kurdistan**.
- Made under a professional artist's guidance with residents — secular and religious, men and women, old and young — as a community pride project.

### 2.3 A Sukkat Shalom kid
*Source: `ראובן פוליטי_סיפור חייו (1).docx` (Yizkor memorial page)*

- Reuven Politi ("Rabbeinu" to his family), born 1954, grew up in **Sukkat Shalom** (one of the Nachlaot courtyards-neighbourhoods), studied at Alliance, repeatedly won the **Tehillim quiz championship at the neighbourhood synagogue**, and was known for original Purim costumes his mother made. Fell on Tel Antar, Golan, Yom Kippur War, 22 Oct 1973.

### 2.4 Yemenite Jews in Jerusalem, 1882
*Source: image `תימנים.jpg` (Nachlaot folder) — Havatzelet newspaper clipping, 26 Av 5642 (1882)*

- Report: Yemenite Jews fled hardship in Yemen; some came to Jerusalem, most settled in Egypt, then left Egypt amid unrest and came to Jerusalem. About **200 souls**, all destitute.
- ⚠ The clipping itself doesn't mention Nachlaot — the link is only that it sits in your Nachlaot folder.
