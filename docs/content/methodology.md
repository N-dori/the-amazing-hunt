# Content methodology

Content work keeps the following layers separate: treatment, interaction atom, question atom, content form, guide move, guide stance, constraint, renderer, and tour-level/logistical design.

Research is converted into visitor experiences through a big idea, outcome, aha, anchor, asks, shows, actions, notices, reveals, and branches. Digital is a renderer, not a pedagogy by itself.

## Authoring safeguards

- Keep provenance attached to every factual claim.
- Label reconstructions, traditions, and attributed quotations accurately.
- Do not invent speech or unsupported demographic, historical, political, or cultural claims.
- Keep opt-out and equivalent accessibility paths real.
- Treat sensitive settings and visitor contribution as context-dependent, with consent and review.

## Stereotype copy style

The `YOU KNOW THE TYPE…` side is a recognition device, not a factual group description. Write it as a person might actually say it in conversation: simple spoken English, short phrases, active voice, recognizable social shorthand, and a light touch of Israeli slang or Hebrew only when it improves recognition.

Use a Hebrew or Israeli term when it opens a useful cultural doorway, and make its meaning clear from the sentence itself. For example: `kippah srugah — a knitted kippah`, `Hiloni — secular`, `Masorti — traditional`, or `Novy God — the FSU-style New Year celebration`.

Avoid academic terminology, sociology language, explanatory paragraphs, over-polished museum copy, unexplained insider slang, degrading or hostile labels, and language that tries too hard to sound Israeli. Do not use intelligence, morality, criminality, loyalty, human worth, or inherent political behavior as recognition shorthand.

Keep the registers deliberately distinct:

- `YOU KNOW THE TYPE…` is conversational, compressed, and may be slightly cheeky.
- `…BUT ACTUALLY,` is still plain English, but calmer, more precise, and evidence-led.

The second side must complicate the first without replacing it with a flattering counter-stereotype. Front-side slang is a small cultural doorway, not a test of insider knowledge.
