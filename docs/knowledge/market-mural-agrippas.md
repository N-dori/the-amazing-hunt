# The market mural on Agripas St (CitéCréation, 2001)

Knowledge for **The Amazing Shuk**: the trompe-l'œil mural of the market on Agripas St, at the corner of Beit Ya'akov St, and the lion riddle "A Hidden Symbol".

## Why it was painted

| Claim | Label | Source | Can serve |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Jerusalem mural circuit (8 murals over 15 years) was meant to attract tourists while political tensions kept them away | Historical fact | M3 | The aha |
| East Jerusalem Development asked for it, with the Ministry of Tourism and the City of Jerusalem; it carried the project out. Partners also: the Israel Government Tourist Corporation and the Ministry of Construction | Historical fact | M1, M3 | Background |
| The area had been under renewal since 1987 | Historical fact | M1 | Background |
| CitéCréation paints in ordinary and social-housing districts, with the residents (in Lyon: the Tony Garnier Urban Museum, 25 murals on an inter-war social-housing district; "priority neighbourhoods") | Historical fact | M6, M7 | The aha: overlooked places, made with the people who live there |
| "It completely transformed the quartier… It has given something back to the residents: pride" (Halim Bensaïd, CitéCréation) | Attributed tradition (CitéCréation's own words) | M6 | A quote, attributed |
| Murals raise property values | **Unknown**: no study found; the pages that claim it cite none | — | **Never an answer or a fact** |

Other CitéCréation work in Jerusalem: "Children of Jerusalem" (ילדי ירושלים) on the Ben Yehuda mall, about 2000 (M8), and a mural on Jaffa St, no year (M4).

## The lion riddle ("A Hidden Symbol")

The owner's 3 Nov decision keeps the reveal to four points (marked ★). The rest is here for other challenges or a deeper reveal, not for this one.

| Claim | Label | Source | Can serve |
|---|---|---|---|
| ★ A keystone over the painted fish shop's arch carries the lion from the Lions' Gate | Historical fact (from the flyer only; whether it's legible from the pavement is a pending field check) | M1 | Step 1: find the lion |
| ★ Judah is "a lion's whelp": גּוּר אַרְיֵה יְהוּדָה (Gen. 49:9) | Historical fact (primary text) | M11 | The reveal |
| ★ The English *Jew* comes from Hebrew *Yehudi* ("of Judah"), through Greek *Ioudaios*, Latin *Iudaeus*, Old French *giu* | Historical fact (Wikipedia, which asks for more citations) | M12 | The reveal |
| ★ Jerusalem's municipal emblem, since 1949, is a lion on a shield; the committee read it as *gur aryeh Yehuda*, later the kingdom of Judah's symbol | Historical fact | M13 | The reveal |
| יהודה → יהודי is a one-letter change (ה → י) | Historical fact (the spelling) | — | Step 2; needs a real sign with יהודה in sight (pending field check) |
| *Yehudim* already names Judah's people in 2 Kings 16:6, before the exile, so *Yehud* (the Persian province, about 539–332 BCE) is not a step between Judah and *Yehudi* | Historical fact | M11, M14 | Keeps the chain correct |
| Mordecai, a Benjaminite, is *ish yehudi* (Esther 2:5): after the kingdom, *Yehudi* meant the whole people | Historical fact (primary text) | M11 | Deeper reveal |
| **Dan is "a lion's whelp" too**: דָּן גּוּר אַרְיֵה (Deut. 33:22) | Historical fact (primary text) | M11 | **Never a "which tribe is the lion?" question; never Dan as an option** |
| The Lions' Gate animals are probably leopards or panthers, the emblem of the Mamluk sultan Baybars (about 1273), later read by Jerusalem's Jews as the Lion of Judah; the gate itself is Suleiman's (about 1538) | Interpretation ("Historians think…") | M1, M15 | A twist |
| Selim I dreamed of lions, and that is why the lion became Jerusalem's symbol | Attributed tradition (a legend, flagged dubious) | M15 | Only as "It's told that…" |
| The Lions' Gate carvings commemorate the Ottoman victory of 1517 | Attributed tradition (sources differ with the Baybars dating) | M15 | Not a fact |
| Mahane Yehuda (the neighbourhood, 1887) is named after Yehuda Navon, Yosef Navon's brother, who died young; the market took the neighbourhood's name | Historical fact | M16, M17 | **No link from the market's name to the lion.** At most: "named after a man called Yehuda; his name is Judah's name too" (Interpretation) |
