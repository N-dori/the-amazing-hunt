# National Library of Israel — Self-Guided Visitor Tour

## Kaplan Street Floor (Level 0)

### Gardens and Building Exterior

Before entering the structure, stop for a moment to look at the beautiful gardens surrounding the Library, visit one of the impressive outdoor works of art, and take in the architectural design of the building itself.

### *Letters of Light* — Micha Ullman

To the left of the entrance, at the edge of Kaplan Street, you’ll find the [*Letters of Light*](https://www.nli.org.il/en/visit/exhibitions/art-works/letters-of-light-artwork) sculpture, by sculptor Micha Ullman. It is situated in the Koum Family Foundation Letters of Light Sculpture Garden, where you will see eighteen blocks of stone excavated from the Ramon Crater, standing in a circle.

At first glance, the letters may not be immediately visible, but soon enough, the letters of the Hebrew alphabet are revealed as sunlight and shadow move through the structures. In the heart of the square, are embedded glass windows, through which you can see the Hebrew letter א (*Alef*), alongside "A" and the Arabic "أ" (*Alif*) projected below.

At the junction of Kaplan Street and Ruppin Road, you can find the entrance to the passage to the sculpture’s underground section, containing an additional three letters – the guttural Hebrew ח, ע, and ה letters. Find more details on the sculpture [here](https://www.nli.org.il/en/visit/letters-of-light).

The *Letters of Light* sculpture, like the entire Library garden, is not fenced. It is open to the broader public and accessible to passersby. However, the underground section is closed outside of Library opening hours.

### Stewart and Lynda Resnick Entrance Plaza

After visiting the sculpture, head back to the main entrance to the Library, the Stewart and Lynda Resnick Entrance Plaza on Kaplan Street.

### Taube Family Bookshop

Once you enter the Stewart and Lynda Resnick Entrance Hall, to your right you will see "[As Promised](https://shop.as-promised.com/en)" at the Taube Family Bookshop. Here, you can buy books, design products, NLI souvenirs and Judaica items. Don’t forget to stop by before completing the tour!

### The Treasures Experience and Exhibitions

Next to the bookshop is the Younes & Soraya Nazarian Experiential Center, which serves as the entrance to "[The Treasures](https://www.nli.org.il/en/visit/tours/exhibition-tickets)" experience. "The Treasures" experience includes the Library's interactive video display and its permanent exhibition, "A Treasury of Words”, as well as the rotating exhibitions. "The Treasures" experience is paid entry.

The permanent exhibition, housed in the William Davidson Permanent Exhibitions Gallery, invites you to set out on a journey through the past via the written word: view ancient holy texts up close, draw inspiration from great writers and artists like S.Y. Agnon and Naomi Shemer, discover the magical spells people once wrote on "incantation bowls,” and gain a deep understanding of how words have shaped our history and culture in this part of the world. Some of the items are accessible thanks to advanced digital technology, which will allow you to flip through centuries-old books and directly experience these rare and valuable items. [You can find more details and order tickets here](https://www.nli.org.il/en/visit/tours/exhibition-tickets).

### Jacqueline Heller Information Desk

At the other end of the Stewart and Lynda Resnick Entrance Hall, right in front of you, is our Jacqueline Heller Information Desk. If you haven’t already ordered your tickets [online](https://www.nli.org.il/en/visit/tours/exhibition-tickets), you can purchase tickets for exhibits and tours at the desk. You can also get an audio guide here, which will accompany you when visiting the interactive video display and the “A Treasury of Words” exhibition.

### View of the Reading Halls

Next to the entrance to the video display and exhibition, you can see the wide glass window looking into the very heart of the Library – the reading halls. **Please note: the reading halls are intended for those who have come to the Library to study. Absolute silence is required when present in the reading halls. **

### Education Center and David Geffen Auditorium

To the left of the Stewart and Lynda Resnick Entrance Hall, you can find the Stephen A. Schwarzman Education Center, which is intended for schoolchildren and closed to the broader public. Next to it is the David Geffen Auditorium, where [musical performances, special events and lectures](https://www.nli.org.il/en/visit/events) are held all year round.

### Cloakroom, Lockers, and Elevators

Just outside the Education Center is a cloak room intended for depositing items. Please note: we also have [free lockers](https://www.nli.org.il/en/at-your-service/services/locker-rentals) available for you. These can also be can be rented for long-term use. The lockers are located on level -1, outside the reading halls.

Opposite the cloak room are the elevators which connect all levels of the Library that are accessible to visitors. They also lead to the Wohl Synagogue and the Anne Hymes Germanacos Prayer Room.

### Harry’s Café and Nearby Artworks

Behind the service and information desk on the Kaplan Street floor is [Harry's Café](https://www.nli.org.il/en/visit/information/cafeteria), where you can snack on sourdough sandwiches baked on site, fresh salads, baked goods, and great coffee. Nearby, you will find a pleasant outdoor patio where you can sit back and enjoy the Jerusalem air. While you're inside Harry's Café, you can observe the unique textile artwork of artist Gali Cnaani, titled [*A Mountain by the Side of the Road*](https://www.nli.org.il/en/visit/exhibitions/art-works/gali-cnani): long walls padded with pillows and woven cloths, softening the rough concrete and adding warmth and a human touch to it, while also absorbing outside noise. Don't forget to stop and enjoy Edmund de Waal’s intriguing artwork, [*psalm, IV*](https://www.nli.org.il/en/visit/exhibitions/art-works/pslam), which is found just outside the entrance to the café.

## Ilona and Hugo Lowy Reading Halls

Now that you know what can be found on the entrance floor (Level 0), you are invited to stop and observe our amazing reading halls.

The Ilona and Hugo Lowy Reading Halls are the crown jewel of the Library. This is the space where you can become acquainted with the books and writings we have collected and stored over the course of 130 years of existence. The reading halls are designed as a well of knowledge – the cylindrical form and semi-transparent ceiling turns skyward, allowing daylight to flood the space and save electricity and energy. 

**Please note that to enter the reading halls, you must first [register on the NLI website and obtain a library card](https://www.nli.org.il/en/at-your-service/entering-the-reading-halls#cardget)** **(free of charge).**

In the reading halls, you can enjoy all the services provided by the Library staff, as befits a leading research library. Every level contains a service desk where you can receive the books you ordered for reading via the [Library catalog](https://www.nli.org.il/en/search?projectName=NLI). At the reference and guidance desks, our staff can help you find the information you’re looking for.

You can read and go through the books you ordered on any of the many reading stations spread throughout the Library – some six hundred in all. Each of them contains an electrical socket, a USB port, and a reading light. In addition, you can [book one of the group study rooms](https://www.nli.org.il/en/at-your-service/study-room-reservation) in advance, which allow for discussion and study in small groups.

### David Berg Foundation Rotunda Gallery

A stairwell on the bottom level of the reading halls descends into the depths of the well of light and knowledge. When you reach the end of this descending spiral staircase, you will find the David Berg Foundation Rotunda Gallery with its large window.

**Please note that individual entry to the rotunda is not permitted, You must be accompanied by a Library guide. Please ask for more details at the Jacqueline Heller Information Desk on floor 0.**

### Automated Library Storeroom

The large window in the rotunda offers a peek into our wondrous treasure trove – the Library’s enormous, automated storeroom.

A maze of high shelves, where four sophisticated robots move back and forth, quietly and efficiently, carrying crates filled with books, based on orders received from the Library's online catalog.

### Special Collections Reading Hall

You can also find the entrance to the Ilona and Hugo Lowy Special Collections Reading Hall here. This is where you can study manuscripts, rare books, archives, antique maps, original sheet music, and other rare items which the Library has collected over the years.

**Note:** Entry to the Special Collections Reading Hall is only permitted to those who have received approval to view a special item, after ordering it using the NLI's online catalog. 

## Level −1 — Reading Hall Lobby

After finishing taking in the reading halls, step out into the lobby on Level -1.

### *Cypresses* — Michal Rovner

Here you'll be able to view the video artwork [*Cypresses*](https://www.nli.org.il/en/visit/exhibitions/art-works/broshim) by artist Michal Rovner, on the wall next to the circulation desk. Rovner photographed cypress trees in the Old City of Jerusalem, erased the background, and imposed them upon a stony landscape on which she added brushstrokes of gold paint.

### Architectural Staircase

Afterwards, make your way to the large staircase and walk downstairs. Once you reach the bottom, stop and have a look up. Take a moment to observe the meticulous design and geometric beauty of the staircase from this vantage point. It represents yet another work of art and is typical of the style for which the building architects are known for.

## Level −2 — Russell Berrie Entrance Hall

### *Broken Time* — Michal Rovner

You are now on level -2 and find yourself in the Russell Berrie Entrance Hall. Nearby you will notice another impressive work of Rovner’s, this one is titled [*Broken Time*](https://www.nli.org.il/en/visit/exhibitions/art-works/broken-time).

This piece is a large stone circle, broken in two, with the appearance of an archaeological artefact marked by the traces of time. A video projection on the stone shows people marching in different directions through arid landscapes, mountains, and ravines, never reaching their goal - while also giving the impression of letters in motion. The work deals with Rovner's characteristic themes: text, migration and displacement, the passage of time - and in this case, an uneven sequence of restarts and endless repetition.

### *Every Hostage Has a Story*

Nearby you can look into the reading halls through the large windows and see our [special display dedicated to the hostages abducted on October 7.](https://www.nli.org.il/en/visit/exhibitions/every-hostage-has-a-story)

Since October 7, we have all been overwhelmed with sadness and perhaps a sense of helplessness in the face of the unimaginable tragedy that has befallen so many families.

Like everyone else, we at the National Library of Israel felt the need to do something. Something that could help people realize that these are human beings, not just numbers and faces on a poster. We wanted to illustrate how there is an entire life behind each of these faces. To do this, we decided to make use of the books that fill our library.

This exhibit is called “Every Hostage Has a Story.” Many dozens of black chairs, far too many, have been placed in our new reading hall. Each chair has a picture of one of the hostages placed on it. There is also one baby chair, as difficult as this is to imagine. Thanks to relatives who have shared stories of their loved ones, we were able to learn a little bit about each of the hostages. Based on this we chose a book for each person that we thought would help others get to know them better and understand who they are. You can read more about this special display [here](https://www.nli.org.il/en/visit/exhibitions/every-hostage-has-a-story).

### National Library Timeline — David Polonsky

On the opposite side of the lobby you are standing in, you can find the National Library of Israel [timeline](https://www.nli.org.il/en/visit/exhibitions/art-works/timline). In 2024, the Library celebrated 132 years of existence, in all of its various manifestations. In order to tell you about the amazing history of this institution, we asked the award-winning and internationally renowned illustrator David Polonsky to create a visual timeline.

Polonsky, whose works include the film *Waltz with Bashir *and the graphic novel *Anne Frank's Diary: The Graphic Adaptation*, put all of his artistic passion into this work. He was able to create a timeline that displays major turning points in the history of the National Library of Israel. The illustrations were inspired by photographs and other items from Library collections.

Polonsky was also inspired by ancient inscriptions found across the globe, from Egypt to South America - reliefs and drawings which appeared on monuments, stelae and victory columns thousands of years ago and which told the stories of historical events using images.

### Wohl Synagogue, Prayer Room, and *sukkah*

Right next to the Saidler Gate Entrance to the building on the Ruppin Road floor, you can see another staircase, which leads down to the Wohl Synagogue and the Anne Hymes Germanacos Prayer Room, as well as an artwork by Edmund de Waal, [*sukkah*](https://www.nli.org.il/en/visit/exhibitions/art-works/suka)*.* It’s worth stepping into the beautiful synagogue as well as the prayer room serving multiple faiths, at the edge of which is a beautiful Armenian-Jerusalemite ceramic work.

### Dairy Restaurant

Finished taking in the artworks? Sit down for a light meal in the courtyard of the dairy restaurant which is on your right when you enter the building. You'll have a wonderful view of the Library gardens. On rainy days, you can enjoy your meal in the restaurant's spacious and pleasant indoor hall.

## Return Through the Gardens

### *Fragments of Ships* — Yechiel Shemi

Afterwards, go through the Saidler Gate Entrance on Ruppin Road, and turn right to the other side of the beautiful garden, where you can find the [*Fragments of Ships*](https://www.nli.org.il/en/visit/exhibitions/art-works/ships) sculpture by artist Yechiel Shemi, which commemorates the hardship – and the hope – of the era of clandestine Zionist immigration. You can also tour the garden itself - the Idan and Batia Ofer Park - which is filled with rich, diverse Mediterranean flora.

After finishing, take the access path in the garden back to Kaplan Street.
